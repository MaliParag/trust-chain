# Offline Find My Cryptography and Anti-Tracking Bounds

## Overview

* **Link in the Chain:** Zero-knowledge crowd-sourced Bluetooth Low Energy (`BLE`) location and physical anti-stalking layer in Part IV; enables offline Apple devices, powered-off iPhones, and `AirTag` or `MFi` accessories to relay encrypted GPS coordinates through stranger devices without revealing beacon identity, finder identity, or plaintext location to Apple.
* **Core Security Invariant:** Offline beacons step an autonomous symmetric `ANSI-X9.63-KDF` ratchet $SK_i = \text{KDF}(SK_{i-1})$ to derive affine scalar pairs $(u_i, v_i)$ and broadcast unlinkable `NIST P-224` public keys $P_i = u_i P_0 + v_i G$ without storing master private scalar $d_0$ on accessory silicon, while passing Finder devices encrypt coordinates via `ECIES` (`AES-128-GCM`) directly to $P_i$ indexed solely by $\text{SHA-256}(P_i)$.
* **Platform Matrix:** Offline beacon emission runs across `iOS` (including Ultra-Wideband and `BLE` power-reserve broadcast after shutdown), `iPadOS`, `macOS` (lid-closed sleep), `watchOS`, `AirPods`, `AirTag`, and `Find My` `MFi` accessories; Finder relay participation spans `iOS`, `iPadOS`, and `macOS`, while cross-network unwanted-tracker alerts (`IETF DULT`) span `iOS 17.5+` and `Android 6.0+`.
* **Trusted Computing Base (TCB):** Owner `SEP` and `Octagon`-synced master `P-224` private scalar $d_0$ plus primary/secondary symmetric seeds ($SK_0$, $SK_{\text{sep},0}$), Bluetooth controller and `AirTag` `nRF` firmware enforcing atomic MAC-and-key rotation, Finder `searchpartyd` `SEC 1` point validation and `ECIES` encryption, and on-device `DULT` trajectory-clustering engines.
* **Failure Horizon & Handoff:** Unauthenticated 31-byte `BLE` broadcast frames permit spoofed `P-224` points (`OpenHaystack`), air-gapped covert data uplinks (*Send My*), and pre-`iOS 18.2` non-root MAC-prefix brute-forcing (`nRootTag`), while fast privacy rotation fundamentally opposes rapid anti-stalking detection; hands off on-device AI and developer agent containment to [Chapter 18](18-agentic-ai-workload-isolation.md).

## Threat Model & Design Tension

Recovering a stolen `MacBook Pro` whose thief immediately disabled Wi-Fi, or locating lost keys attached to a coin-cell `AirTag` lacking cellular radios and GPS receivers, requires recruiting over one billion nearby stranger devices as opportunistic `Bluetooth Low Energy` (`BLE`) relays [53, 461]. Building a planet-scale crowd-sourced RF location network without creating a turnkey mass-surveillance infrastructure forces four conflicting adversarial models into simultaneous tension [53, 461, 462, 463].

First, against *passive physical-layer and link-layer RF observers* (retail analytics sensors, municipal Bluetooth sniffers, and adversaries running software-defined radios along transit corridors [464]), an offline `iPhone`, `iPad`, or `MacBook` in a commuter's bag must never emit a static `BLE` MAC address or static cryptographic identifier that lets observers link Sighting $A$ at 08:30 with Sighting $B$ at 09:00 [53, 461]. Even subtle timing skews between MAC rotation and payload rotation, or physical-layer carrier frequency offsets (`Givehchian et al., IEEE S&P 2022` [464]), must be bounded so passersby remain indistinguishable from background RF noise.

Second, against *curious or compromised Finder relays* (Tier 2/3), a stranger's `iPhone` overhearing a lost-device broadcast must neither learn which Apple Account owns the beacon nor acquire any algebraic trapdoor enabling that stranger to recognize future keys emitted by the same device [53, 461]. Conversely, because `BLE 4.0` legacy non-connectable advertising PDUs carry at most 31 payload bytes over a unidirectional broadcast channel (`ADV_NONCONN_IND` [465]), an energy-constrained offline `AirTag` *cannot perform interactive TLS mutual authentication* with passing Finder phones! Any microcontroller on earth capable of transmitting 31-byte `BLE` packets on 2.4 GHz advertising channels `37`, `38`, and `39` can inject arbitrary curve points into passing iPhones (`OpenHaystack` [466], *Send My* [467], and `nRootTag` [468]).

Third, against *Apple's own cloud infrastructure and legal compulsion* (Tier 4), the backend database receiving billions of daily encrypted location reports must remain cryptographically zero-knowledge [53]: Apple's servers must know neither which `Apple Account` corresponds to a queried key hash, nor the plaintext GPS coordinates inside stored reports, nor which Finder uploaded a given sighting [53, 461].

Fourth, the April 2021 launch of inexpensive $29 `AirTag` trackers elevated *interpersonal physical stalking* to a primary threat model [462, 463, 469, 470]. Observe the fundamental cryptographic paradox at the heart of crowd-sourced tracking [462, 469, 471]: *perfect beacon unlinkability* (rotating keys and `BLE` MAC addresses every 15 minutes unconditionally so no observer can link consecutive broadcasts) simultaneously blinds a stalked victim's smartphone from realizing that *one single hidden tracker planted inside a coat lining or wheel well has followed them across five neighborhoods*! Conversely, freezing a separated tag's identifier for 24 hours so victims' phones can cluster co-traveling trajectories opens a window where thieves or street-level scanners (`Snatcher`, `ACM CCS 2026` [472]) can pinpoint unattended lost items. Every architectural choice across `Find My` and the joint Apple-Google *IETF DULT* (`Detecting Unwanted Location Trackers`) specification navigates that irreducible mathematical boundary [462, 463, 473].

## Silicon & System Mechanics

### Master P-224 Pairing, 31-Byte BLE Packing, and Affine Scalar Blinding

When a user enables `Find My network` on an `iPhone`, `iPad`, or `Mac`, or pairs an `AirTag` / `MFi` accessory [474], the owner device generates a master asymmetric keypair $(d_0, P_0 = d_0 G)$ on the *`NIST P-224` (`secp224r1`)* prime-field elliptic curve ($p = 2^{224} - 2^{96} + 1$, group order $n \approx 2^{224}$, providing 112-bit classical security [475]) alongside a 256-bit (32-byte) symmetric ratchet seed $SK_0$ (plus a secondary seed $SK_{\text{sep},0}$ for separated-state rotation) [53, 461]. Those secrets sync end-to-end encrypted exclusively across the owner's trusted devices inside the `SEP`-anchored `Octagon` `iCloud Keychain` circle ([Chapter 15](15-icloud-hsm-adp.md)) [53, 421].

Why did Apple select `NIST P-224` (`28`-byte coordinates) here when `SEP`, `Image4`, `Passkeys`, and `PQ3` standardize on `NIST P-256` (`32`-byte coordinates)? The answer lies in link-layer bit arithmetic inside the `Bluetooth Core Specification` [461, 465]. Legacy `BLE` non-connectable `ADV_NONCONN_IND` frames carry a 6-byte Advertiser Address (`AdvA`) plus a strict maximum `31`-byte advertising data (`AdvData`) field [465]. Apple Continuity `AdvData` consumes 4 header bytes (`Length`, `0xFF` Manufacturer Specific Data tag, `0x004C` Apple Company ID, and `0x12` Offline Finding type/length byte) plus 2 status/hint bytes, leaving only `25` bytes inside `AdvData` (insufficient even for a 28-byte `P-224` compressed $x$-coordinate, let alone a 32-byte `P-256` key!) [461]. Apple's protocol engineers solved that 31-byte squeeze by *splitting the 28-byte `SEC 1` compressed public key $x(P_i)$ across the `BLE` MAC address itself and the manufacturer payload* [461, 476]:

| BLE Frame Region | Byte Width | Bit / Field Allocation in Apple Offline Finding (`0x004C` Type `0x12`) |
|---|---|---|
| `AdvA` (`BLE` Random Static MAC) | `6 bytes` | `Bits [7:6]` of Byte 0 forced to `0b11` per `BLE` Random Static Address spec [465]; remaining `46 bits` carry `x(P_i)[0..5]` |
| Apple Continuity Header | `4 bytes` | `0x1E` length (`30 B`), `0xFF` Manufacturer Specific, `0x4C 0x00` Apple Inc., `0x12 0x19` Offline Finding type & `25 B` sublength [461] |
| Status Byte | `1 byte` | Battery level, device class (`iPhone`, `Mac`, `AirTag`, `AirPods`), and `DULT`/separated-state flags [461, 470] |
| Public Key Tail `x(P_i)[6..27]` | `22 bytes` | Remaining 22 bytes (`176 bits`) of the 28-byte `NIST P-224` $x$-coordinate [461, 475] |
| Recovered MSBs & Hint | `2 bytes` | Byte 28 stores the `2` top bits of `x(P_i)[0]` overwritten by `0b11` in `AdvA`; Byte 29 reserved/hint (`0x00`) [461] |

![Figure: Offline Find My zero-knowledge location pipeline showing 15-minute unlinkable P-224 key rotation, finder ECIES GPS encryption, blind SHA-256 server indexing, and owner-only private decryption.](diagrams/17-find-my-p224-beacon-flow.svg)

Examine how the beacon derives each rolling public key $P_i$ every 15 minutes once offline (or while running in `iPhone` *Findable After Power Off* low-power Bluetooth/UWB reserve mode after `XNU` shuts down!) [53, 461]:

![Figure: Offline Find My affine P-224 scalar blinding and ECIES report math showing how accessories roll unlinkable public keys without holding master private scalar d_0.](diagrams/17-extra-p224-ratchet-ecies-math.svg)

At each 15-minute tick $i \ge 1$, the Bluetooth microcontroller advances its symmetric chain key via `ANSI-X9.63-KDF` (`SHA-256` counter-mode KDF specified in `SECG SEC 1` [476]) and expands 72 pseudorandom diversifier bytes split into two 36-byte (`288`-bit, providing a 64-bit statistical margin above 224 bits so modular reduction modulo $n$ introduces negligible bias!) integers $(u_i, v_i)$ [53, 461, 476]:

$$SK_i = \text{ANSI-X9.63-KDF}_{\text{SHA-256}}\bigl(SK_{i-1},\; \text{"update"},\; 32\text{ B}\bigr)$$

$$(u_i, v_i) = \text{ANSI-X9.63-KDF}_{\text{SHA-256}}\bigl(SK_i,\; \text{"diversify"},\; 72\text{ B}\bigr) \bmod n$$

From $(u_i, v_i)$, the $i$-th rolling private scalar $d_i$ and corresponding broadcast curve point $P_i$ satisfy an *affine homomorphic blinding* relation over $E(\mathbb{F}_p)$ [53, 461]:

$$d_i = \bigl(u_i \cdot d_0 + v_i\bigr) \bmod n, \qquad P_i = d_i G = u_i P_0 + v_i G$$

Two profound hardware-security properties follow directly from that affine equation [53, 461, 474]. First, *an `AirTag`, `AirPods` case, or third-party `MFi` tracker never stores the master private key $d_0$ in accessory flash or SRAM*! During pairing, the owner's `iPhone` provisions the accessory solely with the public point $P_0$ and symmetric seed $SK_0$. Because $P_i = u_i P_0 + v_i G$ is computed exclusively from public-point scalar multiplications, an attacker who steals an `AirTag` and physically glitches its `nRF52` microcontroller cannot compute past symmetric states $SK_{j < i}$ (blocked by one-way `SHA-256` ratchet forward secrecy) *and* cannot decrypt even current or future location reports because the chip never possessed $d_0$ to form $d_i$! Second, to any RF observer lacking $(P_0, SK_0)$, consecutive points $(P_i, P_{i+1})$ and their derived 46-bit `BLE` MAC addresses are computationally indistinguishable from freshly sampled uniform points on `secp224r1` [53, 461].

### Finder ECIES Encryption and Zero-Knowledge Cloud Retrieval

Whenever any participating `iPhone`, `iPad`, or `Mac` (`searchpartyd`) hears a valid Offline Finding `BLE` advertisement, it reconstructs the 28-byte compressed $x$-coordinate $x(P_i)$ from `AdvA` plus `AdvData`, lifts $x(P_i)$ to an affine curve point $P_i \in E(\mathbb{F}_p)$ (selecting the canonical even/positive $y$-root and verifying $x^3 - 3x + b \equiv y^2 \pmod p$ so invalid off-curve points cannot mount twist/invalid-curve attacks! [476, 477]), and attaches its own recent `CoreLocation` fix [53, 461]. `searchpartyd` samples a fresh single-use ephemeral keypair $(e, Q_e = e G)$ on `P-224`, computes the `ECDH` shared secret $Z = x(e P_i)$, derives a 16-byte `AES-128-GCM` key $K_{\text{enc}}$ and 16-byte initialization vector $IV$ via `ANSI-X9.63-KDF`($Z$, $Q_e \| P_i$), and seals a compact 10-to-16-byte GPS payload (`int32` latitude, `int32` longitude, horizontal accuracy radius, confidence, and status byte) [53, 461, 476]:

$$(K_{\text{enc}}, IV) = \text{ANSI-X9.63-KDF}_{\text{SHA-256}}\bigl(x(e P_i),\; x(Q_e) \,\|\, x(P_i),\; 32\text{ B}\bigr)$$

$$C_{\text{loc}},\, T_{\text{GCM}} = \text{AES-128-GCM.Seal}_{K_{\text{enc}},\, IV}\bigl(\text{lat} \,\|\, \text{lon} \,\|\, \text{acc} \,\|\, \text{conf}\bigr)$$

Notice why `x(P_i)` compression sign ambiguity never breaks `ECDH`: on short-Weierstrass curves, the two square roots $\pm y$ yield opposite points $\pm P_i$, and $e(-P_i) = -(e P_i)$ shares the *exact same $x$-coordinate* $x(e P_i)$! The Finder computes the 32-byte blind lookup index $I_i = \text{SHA-256}(x(P_i))$ and uploads $\langle I_i, t_{\text{upload}}, \text{status}, x(Q_e), C_{\text{loc}}, T_{\text{GCM}} \rangle$ over TLS using anonymous device attestation without attaching the Finder owner's `Apple Account` [53, 461].

Apple's cloud backend stores these reports inside an unindexed key-value store keyed strictly by $I_i = \text{SHA-256}(x(P_i))$. Because `SHA-256` is preimage-resistant and $P_i$ is algebraically blinded, Apple cannot map $I_i$ to $P_0$, link two 15-minute windows $I_i$ and $I_{i+1}$ to the same lost laptop, or decrypt $C_{\text{loc}}$ [53]. When the legitimate owner opens `Find My.app` on another signed-in device (or signs into `icloud.com/find` after completing full two-factor authentication so `Octagon` releases $(d_0, SK_0)$ locally), the client steps $SK_0 \to SK_i$ across the desired search horizon, computes the batch of hashes $\{\text{SHA-256}(x(P_i))\}$, downloads matching ciphertexts from Apple's `acsnservice/fetch` endpoint, derives $d_i = (u_i d_0 + v_i) \bmod n$, recovers $x(d_i Q_e) = x(e d_i G) = x(e P_i)$, and decrypts the device's trajectory locally [53, 461]. In `iOS 18.2+`, Apple extended this exact end-to-end encrypted model to *Share Item Location* [478], allowing a traveler with lost luggage to generate an ephemeral E2EE URL sharing one item's derived decryption window with airline recovery staff, automatically revoking access as soon as the owner reunites with the tag or 7 days elapse [478].

### Academic & Wild Protocol Breaks: OpenHaystack, Send My, and nRootTag

Because offline `BLE` beacons cannot authenticate themselves to stranger iPhones without sacrificing anonymity or exceeding 31 bytes, external security researchers uncovered four remarkable structural attacks against `Find My` between 2020 and 2026 [461, 467, 468, 472].

In 2020-2021, Alexander Heinrich, Milan Stute, Tim Kornhuber, and Matthias Hollick at TU Darmstadt's SEEMOO lab reverse-engineered `searchpartyd` and `FindMyDevice` (*Who Can Find My Devices?*, `PETS 2021` [461] and `OpenHaystack`, `ACM WiSec 2021` [466]). Beyond demonstrating that $5 `ESP32` and `nRF51822` microcontrollers broadcasting arbitrary `secp224r1` points are dutifully relayed worldwide by Apple's Finder fleet, they discovered `CVE-2020-9986` on `macOS Catalina` (`< 10.15.7` Supplemental Update) [461, 479]: `searchpartyd` cached decrypted rolling advertisement keys in a world/user-readable local directory outside `TCC` `CoreLocation` protection! Any unprivileged non-sandboxed `macOS` process could read those cached keys, query Apple's server directly, reconstruct the user's high-precision 7-day location history across *all* their Apple devices without triggering a Location Services prompt, and keep tracking the victim for up to *nine weeks after uninstallation* because `Find My` precomputed future rolling keys nine weeks ahead [461, 479]! They also showed that non-atomic timing gaps between `BLE` MAC rotation and payload rotation allowed continuous RF sniffers to link consecutive 15-minute windows until Apple synchronized rotation atomically in firmware [461].

Months later, Fabian Braunlein at Positive Security demonstrated *Send My* (`2021`) [467], turning `Find My` into a free, global air-gapped data-exfiltration uplink without modifying a single Finder iPhone. Because Apple's key-value store lets any authenticated Apple ID query whether reports exist for any 32-byte hash $\text{SHA-256}(x(P))$, an air-gapped `ESP32` sensor inside a Faraday-shielded or offline facility can encode arbitrary message bits into structured 28-byte arrays `[4b bit_idx | 4b msg_id | 4b modem_id | counter_padding | bit_val]` [467]. Testing candidate counters offline until $x^3 - 3x + b \bmod p$ is a quadratic residue (`Antipa et al.` valid `SEC 1` curve point [477]) ensures passing employees' iPhones accept the point, encrypt dummy GPS reports to it, and upload them to Apple's cloud; a remote `Mac` then queries both `bit = 0` and `bit = 1` key hashes (`256` hashes per 16-byte chunk) to read out arbitrary exfiltrated data at ~3 bytes/second [467]!

Most striking of all, at *USENIX Security 2025*, Junming Chen, Xiaoyue Ma, Lannan Luo, and Qiang Zeng at George Mason University unveiled `nRootTag` [468] (patched by Apple across `iOS 18.2` and `macOS Sequoia 15.2` in December 2024 [480]). On `Linux`, `Windows`, and `Android`, an unprivileged user-space application (`Trojan`) can emit custom `BLE` manufacturer payloads (`AdvData`), *but the host OS Bluetooth stack forbids unprivileged apps from spoofing the 6-byte `AdvA` hardware/random MAC address without root (`CAP_NET_ADMIN`) privileges* [468]! Recall from our framing table above that `Find My` steals its first 46 public-key bits directly from `AdvA`. How could unprivileged malware turn a victim's laptop or `Android` phone into an `AirTag` if it cannot choose those first 46 bits of $x(P)$? `nRootTag` inverted the problem via *cryptographic address-prefix collision* [468]: the unprivileged malware reads the host OS's current `BLE` address (`46` fixed bits), sends that 46-bit prefix to an attacker GPU server (or precomputed `Linux` rainbow tables costing ~$600 on disk, or ~$2.20 in rented cloud GPUs for an online search!), and brute-forces $2^{46}$ scalar multiplications $P = d G$ until finding a valid `P-224` private key $d$ whose public $x$-coordinate starts with the victim laptop's exact 46-bit `BLE` MAC address! Broadcasting the remaining 22 bytes of $x(P)$ inside unprivileged `AdvData` (with the status byte spoofed to `MacBook` to suppress `AirTag` stalking alerts!) let attackers track unrooted laptops, desktops, phones, and even aircraft flight paths within 3 meters via 1.5 billion Finder iPhones until `iOS 18.2` tightened `BLE` address-type and proximity validation [468, 480].

### The Anti-Stalking Paradox and Joint Apple-Google IETF DULT

While `OpenHaystack` and `nRootTag` exploited unauthenticated broadcast ingestion, physical stalking research by Mayberry et al. (*Who Tracks the Trackers?*, `WPES 2021` [469]), Garg et al. (`WiSec 2021` [481]), Heinrich et al. (*AirGuard*, `WiSec 2022` [482]), and Ibrahim et al. (*Track You*, `PETS 2023` [470]) exposed the tension between fast key rotation and stalker detection.

![Figure: Architectural evolution balancing offline beacon unlinkability against unwanted-stalking detection across near-owner rotation, separated-owner AirTag windows, and cross-OS IETF DULT alerts.](diagrams/17-dult-anti-tracking-tradeoff.svg)

To reconcile commuter privacy with victim safety, `AirTag` and `DULT`-compliant trackers run a two-state proximity automaton [53, 462, 463]. In the `Near-Owner` state (receiving periodic authenticated `BLE` keep-alives from the paired owner's phone), the tag steps its primary ratchet every 15 minutes so strangers cannot track the owner. Once disconnected from its owner for ~15-30 minutes (`Separated-From-Owner` state), the tag switches to its secondary seed $SK_{\text{sep}}$, sets an explicit separated-state flag in its status byte, and slows rotation to a *24-hour window* (alongside motion-triggered piezo acoustic chimes at 8-to-24 hours) [462, 463, 470]. That 24-hour stable identifier allows a victim's `searchpartyd` daemon (with heightened nighttime sensitivity documented in *Track You* [470]) to correlate sightings across distinct spatial clusters, push an `"AirTag Found Moving With You"` alert, trigger remote sound playback over `BLE` GATT, or launch `U1`/`U2` Ultra-Wideband *Precision Finding* (`IEEE 802.15.4z` two-way time-of-flight ranging protected against distance-spoofing relays by cryptographic Scrambled Timestamp Sequences) even if an attacker physically desoldered the speaker!

Notice the lingering residual trade-off proved by both academic defenses and attacks [469, 471, 472]: custom `ESP32` and `nRF51` stalker boards (`Mayberry et al.` [469], `Garg et al.` [481], and *Track You* [470]) can clone rotating `AdvA` MAC addresses, cycle fresh `P-224` keys every 15 seconds, or spoof `iPhone`/`AirPod` status-byte nibbles (`0x00` or `MSN = 3`) to evade 24-hour trajectory clustering while continuing to harvest location reports through genuine Finder iPhones (prompting cryptographic *Shamir secret-sharing and Reed-Solomon* proposals by Kaptchuk, Eldridge, et al. [463, 471], where tags broadcast threshold shares every 60 seconds so *only* a receiver co-located with the tag across $k$ consecutive intervals reconstructs a stable linkable token while casual passersby see independent random shares!). Conversely, *Snatcher* (`Ren et al., ACM CCS 2026` [472]) showed that the 24-hour separated key window and unauthenticated sound triggers can expose genuinely *lost* tags in public venues to opportunistic thieves before the owner arrives. Meanwhile, to eliminate the cross-OS blind spot that originally forced `Android` users to install TU Darmstadt's `AirGuard` app [482], Apple and Google co-authored the *IETF DULT* (`draft-ietf-dult-threat-model` and `draft-ietf-dult-tracking-protection-protocol` [462, 463]) standard deployed natively in `iOS 17.5+` and `Android 6.0+` (`May 2024+` [473]), unifying separated-mode `BLE` flags, GATT sound triggers, and NFC serial/masked-owner-phone lookup for law enforcement across all certified trackers.

## Platform Forks Across Apple OSes

While `iOS`, `iPadOS`, and `macOS` share `searchpartyd`, hardware power rails create distinct operational behaviors [53, 461]. Supported `iPhones` (`iPhone 11` through `iPhone 17`) keep their Bluetooth/UWB controller powered in ultra-low-current *Findable After Power Off* reserve mode even after battery depletion or manual shutdown unless the user explicitly toggles off `Find My` on the power-off slider with their passcode [53]. Apple Silicon `MacBooks` broadcast rolling `P-224` beacons during lid-closed sleep (and bind `fmm-mobileme-token-FMM` inside `NVRAM` / `SEP` Activation Lock [340]), whereas `Apple Vision Pro` (`visionOS`) ceases all RF emission immediately upon detaching its external battery pack.

> **Key idea.**
> `OpenHaystack`, *Send My*, `nRootTag`, and *Snatcher* are all consequences of one mathematical conservation law in 31-byte broadcast protocols [461, 467, 468, 472]: *preventing Apple and stranger relays from identifying beacons precludes authenticating beacons at the radio edge, while slowing separated-tag rotation to protect stalking victims inevitably reduces lost-item anonymity.* Sound engineering bounds abuse at the authenticated cloud retrieval gate (`GSA` + `Anisette` rate limits) and standardizes cross-OS `DULT` detection on the victim's phone [53, 463].

On `macOS`, verify that the system `searchpartyd` daemon (`Find My` beacon, Finder relay, and `DULT` tracker evaluation) is running and inspect `NVRAM` `Find My Mac` (`fmm`) Activation Lock state [53, 340]:

```bash
launchctl print system/com.apple.searchpartyd 2>/dev/null | grep -E 'state =|path =' | head -n 4
nvram -p | grep -E 'fmm-computer-name|fmm-mobileme-token-FMM' | cut -c1-60 || true
```

Expected output on a `Find My`-enrolled Mac:

```text
	path = /System/Library/LaunchDaemons/com.apple.searchpartyd.plist
	state = running
fmm-computer-name	MacBook Pro
```

Inspect live `searchpartyd` (`com.apple.icloud.searchpartyd`) `BLE` advertisement scanning, `ECIES` report batching, and unwanted-tracker (`UT` / `DULT`) heuristic evaluations via Unified Logging [53, 274, 463]:

```bash
log show --last 30m --predicate 'subsystem == "com.apple.icloud.searchpartyd"' --style compact | tail -n 4
```

Confirm that `searchpartyd`'s local key caches (the boundary hardened after `CVE-2020-9986` [461, 479]) are locked inside `TCC` / Data Vault directories inaccessible to unprivileged third-party processes:

```bash
ls -ld "$HOME/Library/Caches/com.apple.findmy.fmipcore" "$HOME/Library/Application Support/com.apple.icloud.searchpartyd" 2>/dev/null || true
```
