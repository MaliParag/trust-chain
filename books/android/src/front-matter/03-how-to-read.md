# How to Read This Book

*The Android Trust Chain* develops one continuous architectural argument across twenty numbered chapters organized into four parts, a mobile telemetry and forensics interlude, and a multi-vendor finale, preceded by a [Prologue](../chapters/00-prologue.md) and a [Foundations](../chapters/00b-foundations.md) primer.

## Reading Paths Through the Chain

Readers seeking the complete platform model should read sequentially from [Foundations](../chapters/00b-foundations.md) through [Chapter 20](../chapters/20-multivendor-spyware-chains.md), watching how each software and cloud invariant depends on silicon and kernel properties established beneath it. Part I (*Silicon, Verified Boot, & Hardware KeyMint*, [Chapter 1](../chapters/01-bootrom-avb-vbmeta.md) through [Chapter 4](../chapters/04-keymint-weaver-throttling.md)) establishes immutable hardware roots: SoC Boot ROM fuse verification, bootloader lock states, and `AVB 2.0` `VBMeta` descriptor graphs ([Chapter 1](../chapters/01-bootrom-avb-vbmeta.md)); rollback counters in `RPMB`, `dm-verity` Reed-Solomon Forward Error Correction, `fs-verity`, and `Project Mainline` `APEX` updates ([Chapter 2](../chapters/02-antirollback-verity-apex.md)); shared-silicon `Arm TrustZone` TEEs versus discrete `StrongBox` processors (`Titan M2` and `Knox Vault`) and `Protected Confirmation` ([Chapter 3](../chapters/03-trustzone-strongbox.md)); and `KeyMint` `AuthorizationList` enforcement, boot-state key sealing, and `Weaver` hardware passcode throttling ([Chapter 4](../chapters/04-keymint-weaver-throttling.md)).

Part II (*De-Privileging the Kernel: pKVM, SELinux, & Memory Safety*, [Chapter 5](../chapters/05-pkvm-avf-microdroid.md) through [Chapter 8](../chapters/08-arm-mte-scudo.md)) examines how modern Android contains bugs inside both `Ring 0` Linux and legacy vendor code: `EL2` `Protected KVM` (`pKVM`), `pvmfw`, `OpenProfile for DICE`, and `Microdroid` ([Chapter 5](../chapters/05-pkvm-avf-microdroid.md)); universal enforcing `SELinux`, `CTS` `neverallow` assertions, `ioctl` `allowxperm` filtering, and `Zygote` `seccomp-BPF` ([Chapter 6](../chapters/06-selinux-seccomp.md)); `Project Treble` Binderized `AIDL` HALs, the `Generic Kernel Image` (`GKI`), `KCFI`, Shadow Call Stacks, and Rust across AOSP and kernel drivers ([Chapter 7](../chapters/07-treble-gki-rust.md)); and `ARMv8.5-A`/`ARMv9` Memory Tagging Extension (`MTE`) paired with the `Scudo` hardened allocator ([Chapter 8](../chapters/08-arm-mte-scudo.md)).

Part III (*App Sandbox, Storage, & Local Identity*, [Chapter 9](../chapters/09-uid-zygote-binder.md) through [Chapter 13](../chapters/13-biometrics-passkeys.md)) moves into userspace isolation, data at rest, and human authentication: per-app `AID_*` UIDs, `WebView` `isolatedProcess` sandboxes, and kernel `Binder` object capabilities versus `Intent` confused deputies ([Chapter 9](../chapters/09-uid-zygote-binder.md)); `APK` Signature Schemes `v1` through `v4`, 2026 Android Developer Verification, and `Scoped Storage` ([Chapter 10](../chapters/10-apk-signing-scoped-storage.md)); `AccessibilityService` banking trojans, Restricted Settings, OTP redaction, and Android 16/17 `Advanced Protection Mode` ([Chapter 11](../chapters/11-accessibility-advanced-protection.md)); `fscrypt` v2 `File-Based Encryption`, Direct Boot `BFU`/`AFU` states, physical USB exploit chains, `Identity Check`, and `Theft Detection Lock` ([Chapter 12](../chapters/12-fbe-usb-theft-protection.md)); and `BiometricPrompt` Class 1/2/3 pipelines alongside `Credential Manager` passkeys and `caBLE` proximity transport ([Chapter 13](../chapters/13-biometrics-passkeys.md)).

The Interlude ([Chapter 14](../chapters/14-play-protect-intrusion-logging.md)) explains why Android forbids third-party kernel EDR hooks and how `Google Play Protect` Dynamic Signal Monitoring, enterprise `SecurityLog`, and end-to-end encrypted cloud `Intrusion Logging` provide observation and post-compromise forensics instead. Part IV (*Remote Attestation, Enterprise, & Agentic AI*, [Chapter 15](../chapters/15-key-attestation-rkp.md) through [Chapter 19](../chapters/19-e2ee-backups-private-ai-compute.md)) then extends local silicon proofs to external verifiers, fleets, and AI systems: X.509 `Key Attestation` and `Remote Key Provisioning` ([Chapter 15](../chapters/15-key-attestation-rkp.md)); `Play Integrity` verdicts versus `KernelSU`, `TrickyStore`, and leaked OEM keyboxes ([Chapter 16](../chapters/16-play-integrity-keybox-wars.md)); `Android Enterprise` Work Profiles, `COPE`, and Zero-Touch ([Chapter 17](../chapters/17-android-enterprise.md)); `AICore`, network-less `Private Compute Core`, `AppFunctions`, and `Secure Virtual Window` ([Chapter 18](../chapters/18-on-device-agentic-ai.md)); and `Titan` Cloud Key Vault E2EE backups plus `Private AI Compute` `Titanium Intelligence Enclaves` ([Chapter 19](../chapters/19-e2ee-backups-private-ai-compute.md)). Finally, [Chapter 20](../chapters/20-multivendor-spyware-chains.md) synthesizes complete multi-vendor spyware kill chains and the upstream-to-carrier patch gap.

Practitioners with targeted responsibilities can also enter modularly. Firmware and silicon engineers can focus on [Chapter 1](../chapters/01-bootrom-avb-vbmeta.md) through [Chapter 5](../chapters/05-pkvm-avf-microdroid.md) and [Chapter 15](../chapters/15-key-attestation-rkp.md). Backend and fraud-prevention engineers evaluating device trust should pair [Chapter 4](../chapters/04-keymint-weaver-throttling.md), [Chapter 13](../chapters/13-biometrics-passkeys.md), [Chapter 15](../chapters/15-key-attestation-rkp.md), and [Chapter 16](../chapters/16-play-integrity-keybox-wars.md). Mobile incident responders and high-risk defenders should read [Chapter 11](../chapters/11-accessibility-advanced-protection.md), [Chapter 12](../chapters/12-fbe-usb-theft-protection.md), [Chapter 14](../chapters/14-play-protect-intrusion-logging.md), and [Chapter 20](../chapters/20-multivendor-spyware-chains.md).

## Anatomy of Every Chapter and the Enforcement Tier Coordinate

Every numbered chapter ([Chapter 1](../chapters/01-bootrom-avb-vbmeta.md) through [Chapter 20](../chapters/20-multivendor-spyware-chains.md)) follows an identical six-section progression designed for rapid comparison and reference during design reviews or incident response. Each chapter opens with an outlined *Overview* specification card recording five structural coordinates: `Link in the Chain`, `Core Security Invariant`, `Ecosystem Enforcement Tier`, `Trusted Computing Base (TCB)`, and `Seam & Handoff`. Pay particular attention to `Ecosystem Enforcement Tier`, which distinguishes behavior across three operational bootloader states defined in [Chapter 1](../chapters/01-bootrom-avb-vbmeta.md): `GREEN` (locked bootloader verifying OEM/Google production keys), `YELLOW` (locked bootloader verifying a user-enrolled `avb_custom_key` root, as used by `GrapheneOS`), and `ORANGE` (unlocked bootloader with `AVB` verification bypassed). Recording all three tiers explicitly prevents the common mistake of assuming that unlocking a bootloader degrades only pre-OS boot checks when, in reality, `ORANGE` also alters `KeyMint` root-of-trust key binding and remote attestation claims, whereas `YELLOW` preserves full hardware-enforced Verified Boot and `StrongBox` key isolation under a sovereign root key.

Following the specification card, *Threat Model & Ecosystem Constraint* frames the concrete attack surface and multi-vendor constraint before naming solutions. *Architecture & AOSP Implementation* walks through the exact C, Rust, or AIDL data structures, cryptographic derivations, and kernel execution paths in AOSP, paired with two orthogonal vector diagrams per chapter. *Vendor, OEM, & Custom-OS Boundaries* analyzes how `CDD`/`CTS` mandates, SoC Board Support Packages (`Qualcomm`, `Tensor`, `Exynos`, `MediaTek`), Samsung Knox extensions, and `GrapheneOS` hardenings diverge across real devices. *Exploit Chains & Structural Limits* opens with a standardized six-column historical vulnerability table (`Exploit / Campaign`, `Year`, `Target Layer`, `Entry Vector`, `Root Cause`, and `Mitigation / CTS Gate`) followed by focused post-mortems and mathematical or architectural bounds.

## Notation and Live Device Verification

Cryptographic constructions, key-derivation hierarchies, `DICE` certificate transitions, and probabilistic bounds (such as 4-bit `Arm MTE` tag collision rates and biometric Spoof Acceptance Rates) are typeset in standard TeX notation (`$...$` inline and `$$...$$` displayed), with every symbol defined at point of use. Identifiers from AOSP source trees, Linux kernel subsystems, `AIDL` interfaces, `SELinux` domains, and ASN.1 schemas appear in monospace type, anchored to numbered primary sources in the [References](../back-matter/70-references.md).

Every chapter concludes with *Inspecting via ADB, Fastboot, & Attestation*, containing concise, read-only diagnostic commands (`1` to `4` executable lines per block) using `adb shell`, `fastboot`, `avbtool`, `apksigner`, and `openssl`. Keeping operational commands quarantined inside the closing section preserves uninterrupted conceptual prose while giving engineers immediate, non-destructive verification recipes against Android 14 through Android 17 builds. Domain-specific terms and cross-chapter links are indexed in the [Glossary](../back-matter/80-glossary.md) and [Index](../back-matter/95-index.md).
