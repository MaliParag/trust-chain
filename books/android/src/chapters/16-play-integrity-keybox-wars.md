# Play Integrity Verdicts vs. Root Hiding and Leaked Keyboxes

## Overview

* **Link in the Chain:** Ecosystem attestation-broker and anti-tamper battleground layer; aggregates hardware `Key Attestation` ([Chapter 15](15-key-attestation-rkp.md)), Google Play distribution licensing, and runtime environment telemetry into encrypted `Play Integrity API` verdicts while defending against `Magisk`, `KernelSU`, `APatch`, and `TrickyStore` keybox spoofing.
* **Core Security Invariant:** On Android 13 (`API 33`) and higher, earning `MEETS_DEVICE_INTEGRITY` requires an unrevoked hardware-backed `Key Attestation` certificate proving `deviceLocked == TRUE` and `verifiedBootState == Verified`, while `MEETS_STRONG_INTEGRITY` adds a rolling 12-month security patch floor across both OS and vendor partitions, defeating purely software or kernel-mode root hiding.
* **Ecosystem Enforcement Tier:** Grants `MEETS_DEVICE_INTEGRITY` and `MEETS_STRONG_INTEGRITY` exclusively to Google-certified (`GMS`) `GREEN` locked stock handsets; intentionally withholds `DEVICE` and `STRONG` verdicts from both `YELLOW` locked custom-OS builds (`GrapheneOS`, where `verifiedBootState == SelfSigned`) and `ORANGE` unlocked or rooted builds.
* **Trusted Computing Base (TCB):** `KeyMint` or `StrongBox` paired with `RKP` ([Chapter 15](15-key-attestation-rkp.md)), Google Play Services (`com.google.android.gms.unstable` and the `DroidGuard` virtual machine), the Google Play Integrity cloud verdict issuer, the `/attestation/status` keybox revocation feed, and the relying party's backend token verification endpoint.
* **Seam & Handoff:** Explains why `Binder` interception above `keystore2` (`TrickyStore`) paired with stolen pre-`RKP` OEM factory `keybox.xml` keys temporarily forged `STRONG` verdicts until `RKP` cohorts and revocation waves closed the window, and why sovereign relying parties should verify direct `Key Attestation` to support `YELLOW` hardened OS roots; hands fleet-managed compartmentalization to [Chapter 17](17-android-enterprise.md).

## Threat Model & Ecosystem Constraint

In every chapter from [Chapter 1](01-bootrom-avb-vbmeta.md) through [Chapter 15](15-key-attestation-rkp.md), the physical human holding the handset and the platform security architect stood on the same side against external thieves, network eavesdroppers, and malware authors. Across commercial consumer services, including competitive mobile multiplayer games, Widevine L1 media streaming, ride-hailing driver dispatch, limited-inventory ticketing, promotional coupon disbursement, and retail fintech applications, that threat model inverts completely [45, 111]. Here, the adversary is frequently the *physical owner of the smartphone* or an industrialized click-farm operator running racks of five hundred USB-controlled handsets. That operator intentionally unlocks the bootloader into the `ORANGE` state, patches the Linux kernel or `init` ramdisk, injects `Frida`, `Zygisk`, or `LSPosed` hooks into target processes to spoof GPS coordinates, automate UI taps, or bypass client-side logic, and attempts to convince a remote relying-party server that every API request originated from an unmodified `GREEN` consumer handset [111, 447, 448].

Google's first ecosystem-wide answer to owner-controlled tampering, the `SafetyNet Attestation API` (`2015-2024`, deprecated and shut down by January 2025 [449, 450]), suffered from an architectural inversion of privilege that fueled a decade-long cat-and-mouse war. As Mulliner and Kozyrakis initially mapped [450] and Romain Thomas later reverse-engineered at instruction level [447], `SafetyNet` delegated device inspection to `DroidGuard`, a native C++ virtual machine running inside the isolated `com.google.android.gms.unstable` process. On each attestation run, `DroidGuard` downloaded a freshly randomized, per-request proprietary bytecode program from Google's servers and executed it across a custom virtual CPU containing 256 typed registers and roughly one hundred Mixed Boolean-Arithmetic (`MBA`)-obfuscated instruction handlers, accumulating environment measurements into a BoringSSL `HMAC-SHA256`-authenticated `DroidGuardResult` register [447]. Those bytecode probes inspected `/system/bin/su`, `/proc/self/mounts`, `/proc/self/maps`, `SELinux` enforcement flags, debuggers, and `ro.boot.*` system properties. Crucially, whenever hardware-backed `KeyMaster` attestation was absent or threw a Java `ProviderException`, `SafetyNet` (`ctsProfileMatch` and `basicIntegrity`) silently degraded to evaluating those Normal World `EL0` software heuristics alone [447, 451]. Because `DroidGuard` executed at `EL0` *above* an attacker-controlled `EL1` Linux kernel on an `ORANGE` unlocked phone, every filesystem entry, property table, or system-call return buffer that `EL0` bytecode could read, `EL1` kernel hooks or `Zygote` injectors could forge.

Replacing `SafetyNet` with the `Play Integrity API` and anchoring modern verdicts in hardware `Key Attestation` ([Chapter 15](15-key-attestation-rkp.md)) closed the software-heuristic loophole on Android 13 and higher, yet simultaneously hardened a contentious ecosystem policy coupling [20, 452, 453]. By bundling three orthogonal properties into one opaque verdict, namely *silicon verified boot state*, *Google Play Store distribution licensing*, and *OEM Google Mobile Services (`GMS`) compatibility certification*, `Play Integrity` treats an owner-hardened `YELLOW` operating system identically to a compromised `ORANGE` device. A journalist or security engineer running `GrapheneOS` on a Pixel 9 or Pixel 10 with a re-locked bootloader (`deviceLocked == TRUE`), full `AVB 2.0` `dm-verity`, hardware `Titan M2` `StrongBox` key storage, hardened `malloc`, and synchronous `Arm MTE` possesses demonstrably stronger exploit resistance than almost any stock OEM handset in circulation, yet fails `MEETS_DEVICE_INTEGRITY` because `GrapheneOS` signs `vbmeta` with its own `avb_custom_key` (`verifiedBootState == SelfSigned`) rather than Google's stock GMS release key [20, 453]. Understanding both the cryptographic mechanics of `Play Integrity` and the underground root-hiding escalation explains why that trade-off exists and when relying parties should decouple silicon trust from app-store certification.

## Architecture & AOSP Implementation

### From `SafetyNet` to the `Play Integrity API`: Standard vs. Classic Flows and `JWE`+`JWS` Verdicts

The `Play Integrity API` (`com.google.android.play:integrity`) replaces client-interpretable JWTs with an opaque nested cryptographic token formatted under `RFC 7516` JSON Web Encryption (`JWE`, using `A256KW` key wrapping around an `A256GCM` content cipher) encapsulating an `RFC 7515` JSON Web Signature (`JWS`, signed with `ES256` ECDSA) [45, 454, 455]. Because the outer `JWE` layer is encrypted under keys unknown to the Android handset, neither the calling application nor an on-device `Frida` script can read or mutate the verdict locally; the Android client must forward the opaque token to the developer's backend server, which either calls Google's `playintegrity.googleapis.com/v1/{packageName}:decodeIntegrityToken` endpoint over authenticated TLS or decrypts and verifies the `JWE`/`JWS` envelope locally inside a hardened server environment using Cloud Console-managed keys [454, 456, 457].

To reconcile cryptographic freshness with mobile network latency, `Play Integrity` splits token acquisition into two distinct request architectures [456, 458]. Under *Standard API Requests* (`StandardIntegrityManager`, the default architecture for everyday API mutations), the application performs an asynchronous one-off warm-up during cold start or background initialization by calling `prepareIntegrityToken(PrepareIntegrityTokenRequest)` with its Google Cloud project number (rate-limited to 5 warm-ups per minute per app instance) [458]. During warm-up, Google Play Services performs heavy hardware `Key Attestation` and `DroidGuard` evaluation against Google's backend once and caches a protected, short-lived cryptographic handle on the device (`StandardIntegrityTokenProvider`). Thereafter, whenever the user initiates a sensitive action, the app invokes `integrityTokenProvider.request(StandardIntegrityTokenRequest)` passing a `requestHash` (up to 500 bytes), which mints a fresh request-bound token on-device in a few hundred milliseconds without blocking on a fresh attestation round-trip while retaining automatic Play-managed replay mitigation [456, 458]. Conversely, *Classic API Requests* (`IntegrityManager.requestIntegrityToken`, capped at 5 requests per minute) omit pre-warming and trigger a full live server recomputation of every device, app, and account signal on demand over several seconds, requiring the developer's server to generate and track a single-use URL-safe Base64 `nonce` (`16` to `500` characters) [456].

Once decrypted on the relying party's backend, the verified JSON payload exposes five structured signal dictionaries [454]:

```json
{
  "requestDetails": {
    "requestPackageName": "com.example.bank",
    "requestHash": "e3b0c44298fc1c149afbf4c8996fb924...",
    "timestampMillis": "1747312809120"
  },
  "appIntegrity": {
    "appRecognitionVerdict": "PLAY_RECOGNIZED",
    "packageName": "com.example.bank",
    "certificateSha256Digest": ["6a6a1474b5cbbb2b1aa57e0bc3..."],
    "versionCode": "418"
  },
  "accountDetails": { "appLicensingVerdict": "LICENSED" },
  "deviceIntegrity": {
    "deviceRecognitionVerdict": ["MEETS_BASIC_INTEGRITY", "MEETS_DEVICE_INTEGRITY", "MEETS_STRONG_INTEGRITY"],
    "deviceAttributes": { "sdkVersion": 36 },
    "recentDeviceActivity": { "deviceActivityLevel": "LEVEL_1" }
  },
  "environmentDetails": {
    "appAccessRiskVerdict": { "appsDetected": ["KNOWN_INSTALLED"] },
    "playProtectVerdict": "NO_ISSUES"
  }
}
```

Every field in that JSON structure closes a specific real-world bypass vector [454, 457, 458]. Inside `requestDetails`, the backend must verify three invariants simultaneously: `requestPackageName` equals the expected application ID (defeating cross-app token substitution where an attacker obtains a valid token from a benign game on a clean phone and submits it to a banking API), `currentTime - timestampMillis` falls within a tight freshness window (such as `60` seconds), and `requestHash` (or Classic `nonce`) matches the server's independently recomputed `SHA-256` digest over the canonicalized transaction parameters (`action || recipient_id || amount || session_id`) [111, 454, 458]. Notice why hashing the exact transaction payload is non-negotiable: if a developer passes only a random server nonce without binding the HTTP request body, a man-in-the-middle proxy on a modified device can forward the random nonce to a genuine untouched phone, obtain a valid `STRONG` token for that nonce, and attach it to a mutated HTTP POST transferring funds to an attacker account [111, 458].

Next, `appIntegrity` and `accountDetails` bind binary provenance and user entitlement [454]. A verdict of `PLAY_RECOGNIZED` proves that the calling APK's signing certificate SHA-256 digest and binary distribution match Google Play's catalog (`UNRECOGNIZED_VERSION` flags repackaged, debug-signed, or sideloaded builds), while `LICENSED` confirms that the signed-in Google account acquired an entitlement from Google Play (`UNEVALUATED` appears whenever device trust is too low to vouch for userspace package claims) [454, 457].

Inside `deviceIntegrity`, Google executed a major architectural cutover announced in December 2024 and enforced universally across all integrations in May 2025 for devices running Android 13 (`API 33`) and higher [452, 454]. By transitioning Android 13+ evaluations to rely directly on Platform `Key Attestation` (`OID 1.3.6.1.11129.2.1.17`, plus `KeyMint 4.0` `Tag::MODULE_HASH` `724` binding Mainline APEX module measurements on Android 16+ [29, 459]) rather than shipping sprawling userspace telemetry blobs, Google reduced server-evaluated device signals by roughly 90 percent and cut verdict latency by up to 80 percent while eliminating legacy software fallbacks [452]. Under the post-May-2025 lattice on Android 13+, `MEETS_BASIC_INTEGRITY` requires only that the attestation root of trust chains to Google (allowing unlocked `ORANGE` bootloaders and uncertified builds passing basic anti-hook checks); `MEETS_DEVICE_INTEGRITY` strictly mandates hardware-backed proof that `deviceLocked == TRUE` and the booted OS is a certified OEM GMS image; and `MEETS_STRONG_INTEGRITY` requires `MEETS_DEVICE_INTEGRITY` *plus security updates installed within the trailing 12 months across every partition*, checking both the core Android OS patch level (`osPatchlevel`) and the vendor/boot partition patch levels (`vendorPatchlevel` and `bootPatchlevel`) [452, 454]. Because legacy Android 12 and lower devices still permitted `MEETS_STRONG_INTEGRITY` without the 12-month patch window, `deviceAttributes.sdkVersion` exposes the attested API level so backends can enforce `sdkVersion >= 33` alongside `MEETS_STRONG_INTEGRITY` [452, 454]. Two optional anti-fraud fields further harden `deviceIntegrity`: `recentDeviceActivity` buckets hourly per-app token minting rates from `LEVEL_1` ($\le 10$ Standard or $\le 5$ Classic requests/hour) up to `LEVEL_4` ($> 50$ Standard or $> 15$ Classic requests/hour) to expose clean phones enslaved as automated token-signing farms, while `deviceRecall` lets backends persist three server-side boolean bits (`bitFirst`, `bitSecond`, `bitThird`) with `YYYYMM` UTC write dates tied to hardware identity across app reinstalls and factory resets [454, 460].

Finally, `environmentDetails` bridges remote attestation back to the UI-layer banking-trojan threat model of [Chapter 11](11-accessibility-advanced-protection.md) [454, 457]. When enabled in Play Console (and supported in Standard requests from library `v1.4.0`+ unless skipped via `verdictOptOut`), `appAccessRiskVerdict.appsDetected` reports whether concurrent apps hold active screen-recording (`MediaProjection`), `SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW` overlays, or device-controlling `AccessibilityService` grants, prefixing Play-installed or `FLAG_SYSTEM` preloads with `KNOWN_` (`KNOWN_CAPTURING`, `KNOWN_CONTROLLING`, `KNOWN_OVERLAYS`) and sideloaded packages (or apps running inside another profile such as a Work Profile) with `UNKNOWN_` (`UNKNOWN_CAPTURING`, `UNKNOWN_CONTROLLING`, `UNKNOWN_OVERLAYS`), while exempting assistive screen readers that passed Google Play's `isAccessibilityTool` review [454, 457].

### The Root-Hiding Escalation: `Magisk`, `Shamiko`, `KernelSU`, `APatch`, and `PlayIntegrityFix`

Why did underground root-hiding frameworks evolve through four architectural generations between 2016 and 2026? Tracing each generation reveals a crisp lesson in privilege-ring boundaries [448, 451, 461, 462, 463, 464]:

![Figure: Dual-rail escalation timeline comparing root-hiding and keybox-spoofing tools against Android hardware attestation and RKP defenses.](diagrams/16-play-integrity-vs-root-hiding.svg)

*Generation 1 (`Magisk` Mount Namespaces and `resetprop`, 2016-2021)* emerged directly from `AVB` `dm-verity` ([Chapter 2](02-antirollback-verity-apex.md)). Because modifying `/system` or `/vendor` triggered `dm-verity` faults, John Wu's `Magisk` unlocked the bootloader (`ORANGE`), patched `magiskinit` into `boot.img`'s ramdisk, and constructed a `tmpfs` and `overlayfs` mirror mounting root binaries over `/system/bin` at boot [448]. To hide from `DroidGuard` and banking checks, `MagiskHide` entered target app mount namespaces (`CLONE_NEWNS`), lazily detached (`umount2` with `MNT_DETACH`) every `magisk` overlay, and ran `resetprop` (directly rewriting `init`'s `/dev/__properties__` shared-memory `prop_area` hybrid trie in RAM) to spoof read-only boot properties (`ro.boot.verifiedbootstate=green`, `ro.boot.vbmeta.device_state=locked`, `ro.debuggable=0`) [448]. After `MagiskHide` gave way to `Zygisk` and `DenyList`, closed-source modules led by `Shamiko` extended mount unmounting and scrubbed `Zygote` `dlopen` residues and `/proc/self/maps` entries [448, 461]. `DroidGuard` and commercial RASP SDKs countered with inline Arm64 `svc #0` syscalls bypassing `bionic` PLT hooks, mount-ID gap checks in `/proc/self/mountinfo`, and `ART` JNI table inspection [447, 465].

*Generation 2 (`PlayIntegrityFix` Legacy Downgrade Spoofing, 2022-2025)* exploited backward compatibility rather than hiding root alone [451]. Even when `Shamiko` hid userspace `Magisk`, an `ORANGE` phone's hardware `KeyMint` TEE still reported `deviceLocked == FALSE`. Prior to May 2025, however, `Play Integrity` retained a fallback path for devices launched around Android 7 or 8 whose early `KeyMaster` HALs lacked reliable attestation. Modules such as `chiteroman`'s `PlayIntegrityFix` (`PIF`) injected `Zygisk` hooks into `com.google.android.gms.unstable`, intercepting `KeyStoreSpi.engineGetCertificateChain()` to throw `UnsupportedOperationException` while overwriting `android.os.Build.FINGERPRINT`, `PRODUCT`, `MODEL`, and `ro.product.first_api_level` to impersonate harvested pre-2019 fingerprints for which Google's server still granted `MEETS_DEVICE_INTEGRITY` via software evaluation [451]. Google banned scraped `PIF` fingerprints continuously and closed the loophole structurally in May 2025 by requiring hardware `Key Attestation` for `MEETS_DEVICE_INTEGRITY` on all Android 13+ runtimes [451, 452].

*Generation 3 (`KernelSU`, `APatch`, and `SUSFS`, 2023-2026)* evacuated root out of userspace `Zygote` and mount overlays altogether [462, 463, 465]. Built by `tiann` (`weishu`), `KernelSU` embeds root gating directly inside the `EL1` Linux kernel (compiled into a custom `GKI` `boot.img` or loaded via `init_boot.img` `LKM` hooking `do_faccessat`, `do_execveat_common`, and `vfs_statx`) [462]. On devices without kernel source releases, `bmax121`'s `APatch` (`KernelPatch`) resolves internal kernel symbols via `kallsyms` inside a raw `boot.img` binary and patches Arm64 function prologues inline [463]. Under both designs, `/system/bin/su` exists on no mount table: when an un-allowlisted app or `DroidGuard` invokes `stat("/system/bin/su")` or `execve("su")` via raw `svc #0`, the `EL1` kernel sees an unauthorized `current_uid()` and returns `-ENOENT` natively, while `simonpunk`'s `SUSFS` (`susfs4ksu`) hides custom mount IDs and kernel threads inside VFS before `copy_to_user()` [462, 465]. Yet an immutable hardware wall stops `KernelSU` and `APatch`: modifying `boot.img` or `init_boot.img` invalidates the OEM `AVB` signature, requiring an `ORANGE` unlocked bootloader that `ABL` latches into `TrustZone` / `StrongBox` (`deviceLocked = FALSE`, `verifiedBootState = Unverified`) before `EL1` ever boots.

### `TrickyStore`, Leaked OEM Keyboxes, and the `Binder` Seam Above `keystore2`

Blocked from forging `GREEN` boot state inside `TrustZone` or `Titan M2` silicon, *Generation 4 (`TrickyStore`, 2024-2026)* pivoted to the unencrypted `Binder` IPC seam sitting above `KeyMint` in Normal World between client apps and Android's Rust `keystore2` daemon (`system/security/keystore2`) [464, 466].

![Figure: Sequence of TrickyStore Binder interception above keystore2, forging X.509 Key Attestation leaf certificates with stolen factory keyboxes until CRL revocation.](diagrams/16-extra-trickystore-keystore2-interception.svg)

Examine how `5ec1cff`'s `TrickyStore` subverts attestation without touching Secure World SRAM [464, 466]. Running as `UID 0`, `TrickyStore` injects a `Binder` interceptor monitoring `android.system.keystore2.IKeystoreSecurityLevel` calls (`generateKey` and `createOperation`) from target packages in `/data/adb/tricky_store/target.txt` (`com.google.android.gms` and `com.android.vending`) [464, 466]. When `DroidGuard` requests an attested keypair with `Tag::ATTESTATION_CHALLENGE`, `TrickyStore` takes one of two paths [464]. In *Leaf-Hack Mode* (when hardware TEE still runs), `TrickyStore` forwards `generateKey()` to `KeyMint`, intercepts the `KeyMetadata` reply, parses the leaf certificate's `OID 1.3.6.1.4.1.11129.2.1.17` `KeyDescription`, rewrites `RootOfTrust` in RAM so `deviceLocked = TRUE`, `verifiedBootState = Verified (0)`, and `osPatchlevel`, `vendorPatchlevel`, `bootPatchlevel`, plus `KeyMint 4.0`'s `moduleHash` (`Tag 724` [459]) match current stock OTA values, and re-signs that doctored leaf in software using `/data/adb/tricky_store/keybox.xml` [464]. In *Generate-Key Fallback Mode* (for devices with blown TEE fuses), `TrickyStore` generates the keypair in BoringSSL software, synthesizes the full X.509 chain from `keybox.xml`, and emulates `IKeystoreOperation` signatures in RAM [464, 466].

Why would `Play Integrity` (or any X.509 `Key Attestation` verifier) ever accept a software signature produced by `TrickyStore`? Only when `keybox.xml` holds a *real, unrevoked Google-chained OEM factory attestation private key* ($sk_{\text{OEM\_batch}}$) leaked or stolen from an ODM manufacturing line or vulnerable legacy `TEE` [108, 464]. Prior to mandatory `RKP`, Google provisioned OEMs with intermediate-signed XML keyboxes holding raw `ECDSA P-256` and `RSA-2048` private keys. Because signature verification tests only $\mathrm{Verify}_{pk_{\text{batch}}}(\text{TBS\_Leaf}, \sigma) = 1$ under Google's Hardware Attestation Root CA, a leaf signed in `EL0` RAM with a leaked $sk_{\text{batch}}$ is mathematically indistinguishable from genuine `TEE` output *until $sk_{\text{batch}}$ is revoked*.

Google's platform team countered the `keybox.xml` underground through dynamic revocation telemetry and silicon `RKP` cutoffs [108, 109, 452]. Backend anomaly detectors correlate batch certificate serials against request volume and model diversity: whenever a single regional ODM keybox suddenly signs `MEETS_STRONG_INTEGRITY` requests across thousands of unrelated phones, Google shadowbans the chain and appends its serial to `https://android.googleapis.com/attestation/status` (`KEY_COMPROMISE`) [108, 452]. Simultaneously, mandatory `RKP` across Android 13-16+ ([Chapter 15](15-key-attestation-rkp.md) [109]) eliminates factory XML keyboxes permanently by deriving attestation keys from per-die `UDS` secrets sealed inside silicon.

### Relying-Party Architecture: Brokered `Play Integrity` vs. Sovereign `Key Attestation`

Even when hardware attestation holds, relying parties frequently undermine verification in backend code. In *SafetyNOT* (`ACM MobiSys 2023` [111]), Ibrahim, Imran, and Bianchi audited thousands of production apps and uncovered widespread integration failures: decoding verdicts client-side inside patchable Java methods, omitting `requestPackageName` checks, accepting static nonces, and checking attestation once at login while leaving subsequent bearer tokens unbound to hardware keys.

![Figure: Relying-party trade-off between brokered Google Play Integrity verdicts and sovereign direct X.509 Key Attestation verification.](diagrams/16-relying-party-verification-choice.svg)

> **Warning.**
> Neither `Play Integrity` nor one-shot `Key Attestation` stops a *Real-Phone Signing-Oracle Relay* ("token farm") unless the attested key is cryptographically bound to ongoing session requests [111, 438, 457]. If an API merely sends a challenge `c` and accepts any valid `Play Integrity` token over `c`, a bot farm running automated Python scripts on a cloud VM can proxy `c` over USB to one untouched USD 80 `GREEN` Android phone sitting in a rack, harvest a genuine `MEETS_STRONG_INTEGRITY` token, and replay the resulting session cookie from the server farm. Defeating relay farms requires binding `requestHash = SHA-256(method || path || body_sha256 || dpop_jkt)` *and* requiring every subsequent API call to carry an `RFC 9449` Demonstrating Proof-of-Possession (`DPoP`) or mTLS signature generated inside the handset's non-exportable `KeyMint`/`StrongBox` keypair, paired with `recentDeviceActivity` (`LEVEL_1`/`LEVEL_2`) rate ceilings [438, 454].

## Vendor, OEM, & Custom-OS Boundaries

Across the Android ecosystem, `Play Integrity` draws a hard boundary between GMS-certified `GREEN` consumer ROMs, non-GMS regional forks, and security-hardened `YELLOW` operating systems [20, 453, 454]. Handsets sold in mainland China without GMS, Huawei HarmonyOS builds, Amazon Fire OS tablets, and de-Googled AOSP builds cannot mint `Play Integrity` verdicts at all.

On `GrapheneOS` (`YELLOW` verified boot on Google Pixel hardware), users can optionally run unmodified Google Play Services (`com.google.android.gms`) and Play Store inside *Sandboxed Google Play* [20, 453]. Unlike stock GMS ROMs (where Play Services executes as a privileged preload holding system permissions and SELinux exemptions), Sandboxed Google Play confines `GMS` inside a standard `u0_a*` `untrusted_app` sandbox [20, 453]. When an app invokes `Play Integrity` on `GrapheneOS`, Pixel's `Titan M2` `StrongBox` truthfully signs `deviceLocked == TRUE`, `verifiedBootState == SelfSigned (1)`, and `verifiedBootKey == SHA-256(GrapheneOS_AVB_Release_Key)`. Because `Play Integrity` restricts `MEETS_DEVICE_INTEGRITY` and `MEETS_STRONG_INTEGRITY` to OEM GMS builds (`verifiedBootState == Verified (0)`), `GrapheneOS` receives at most `MEETS_BASIC_INTEGRITY`, causing apps that blindly hard-block on `MEETS_DEVICE_INTEGRITY` to lock out locked `Titan M2`-backed `GrapheneOS` users while pushing tinkerers toward leaked `TrickyStore` keyboxes [453, 464].

For government eID wallets, enterprise Zero Trust IdPs, and high-assurance banks, `GrapheneOS` [453] and AOSP `Key Attestation` ([Chapter 15](15-key-attestation-rkp.md) [29]) support a cleaner architecture: *Sovereign Direct Hardware Key Attestation*. By parsing X.509 `OID 1.3.6.1.4.1.11129.2.1.17` directly on the backend (verifying Google's Hardware Attestation Root CA, polling `/attestation/status`, requiring `StrongBox`/`TrustedEnvironment` in `teeEnforced`, checking partition patch levels, requiring `deviceLocked == TRUE`, and accepting either `verifiedBootState == Verified` or `SelfSigned` paired with `GrapheneOS`'s published per-Pixel `verifiedBootKey` digests), relying parties eliminate cloud broker quotas, block `Magisk` and `KernelSU` identically, and support locked `GrapheneOS` handsets natively [20, 29, 108, 453].

### The Physical-Owner Bound and Handoff to Enterprise Fleet Control

Even after `RKP`, `Titan M2` `StrongBox`, and the May 2025 `Play Integrity` hardware mandate eliminate forged boot states and leaked factory keyboxes, a fundamental information-theoretic ceiling remains: *remote attestation bounds software integrity, never physical reality outside the sensors* [111, 457]. An adversary willing to buy physical `GREEN` smartphones, mount capacitive stylus actuators and cameras over their screens, or pay human click-farm workers can always generate genuine hardware-attested tokens at physical device speed, requiring relying parties to pair hardware attestation with `recentDeviceActivity` velocity limits, `deviceRecall` ban persistence, `DPoP` session binding, and server-side behavioral anomaly detection [438, 454, 460].

In consumer apps, the device owner is an autonomous external party whose phone the relying party may distrust yet never administer. Inside a corporation, hospital, or government agency, the operational requirement changes fundamentally: the organization must *cryptographically partition* corporate applications, networks, and `fscrypt` encryption keys away from an employee's personal life on the same physical handset while enforcing hardware-bound `Zero-Touch` fleet compliance. [Chapter 17](17-android-enterprise.md) examines how `Android Enterprise` constructs that multi-user boundary inside the kernel and framework.

## Inspecting via ADB, Fastboot, & Attestation

Inspect Google Play Services (`com.google.android.gms`) and its isolated `DroidGuard` attestation VM process (`com.google.android.gms.unstable`) alongside SELinux domains on a connected GMS handset:

```bash
adb shell ps -AZ | grep 'com.google.android.gms' | head -n 6
```

Query the exact boot-state and multi-partition security patch-level properties (`OS` vs. `vendor` vs. `boot`) that must fall within a trailing 12-month window to satisfy `MEETS_STRONG_INTEGRITY` on Android 13+:

```bash
adb shell getprop ro.boot.vbmeta.device_state
adb shell getprop ro.build.version.security_patch
adb shell getprop ro.vendor.build.security_patch
adb shell getprop ro.boot.os_patch_level
```

Check `ro.product.first_api_level` (`>= 33` indicates mandatory `RKP` hardware launch baseline) and verify whether the `rkpd` Remote Key Provisioning service is registered:

```bash
adb shell getprop ro.build.fingerprint
adb shell getprop ro.product.first_api_level
adb shell service list | grep -i rkp
```

Fetch Google's live keybox revocation list (`attestation/status`) from your workstation and count currently revoked compromised or leaked attestation serial entries:

```bash
curl -fsSL https://android.googleapis.com/attestation/status | grep -c '"status"'
```
