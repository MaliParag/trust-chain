# Android Enterprise: Work Profiles, COPE, and Zero-Touch

## Overview

* **Link in the Chain:** Multi-user cryptographic profile compartmentalization and fleet-management layer; isolates corporate applications, networks, and `fscrypt` keys inside a managed `UserHandle` (`Work Profile`) or full-device `DevicePolicyManager` boundary anchored by `Zero-Touch Enrollment`.
* **Core Security Invariant:** A managed `Work Profile` (`user 10+`) executes every corporate app under distinct kernel `UID`s (`100000 * u + appId`) and encrypts `/data/user/10` under an independent `File-Based Encryption` master key $K_{\text{CE}, 10}$, allowing an Enterprise Mobility Management (`EMM`) controller to pause (`evict key`) or cryptographically wipe corporate data without reading or harming `User 0` personal data.
* **Ecosystem Enforcement Tier:** Multi-user `UserHandle`, separate `fscrypt` profile keys, `CrossProfileIntentFilter`, and `DevicePolicyManager` APIs enforce identically across AOSP `GREEN` and `YELLOW` (`GrapheneOS` users routinely leverage secondary profiles and open-source `Shelter`/`Insular` Work Profiles for compartmentalization!); cloud `Zero-Touch Enrollment` and Managed Google Play bind to GMS (`GREEN`) fleets (paralleled by Samsung `Knox Mobile Enrollment`).
* **Trusted Computing Base (TCB):** `UserManagerService` (`UserHandle` UID arithmetic), `vold` + `KeyMint` per-user `CE`/`DE` key derivation ([Chapter 12](12-fbe-usb-theft-protection.md)), `DevicePolicyManagerService` (`frameworks/base/services/devicepolicy/`), `PackageManager` `CrossProfileIntentFilter`, and the installed Device Policy Controller (`DPC` / Android Management API agent).
* **Seam & Handoff:** Reconciles corporate data loss prevention (`DLP`), per-profile VPNs, and instant offboarding wipes with cryptographic employee privacy on BYOD and `COPE` devices; transitions to [Chapter 18](18-on-device-agentic-ai.md) to examine how modern Android isolates cross-app Agentic AI (`AICore`, `Private Compute Core`, and `AppFunctions`).

## Threat Model & Ecosystem Constraint

Legacy mobile device management (`Device Administrator` API introduced in Android 2.2 and deprecated in Android 9/10) suffered from an intractable all-or-nothing design flaw: enrolling an employee's personal smartphone in corporate email granted the employer's MDM agent sweeping device-wide authority (`wipeData()` factory-resetting the employee's baby photos!) while simultaneously leaving corporate attachments sitting inside the exact same `/sdcard` and clipboard namespace as the employee's personal games and social-media apps [121, 122]. Employees rightly refused enrollment out of privacy fear; CISOs rightly distrusted unmanaged personal apps sitting next to corporate OAuth tokens and VPN tunnels.

Designing a genuine enterprise architecture (`Android Enterprise`, introduced as *Android for Work* in Android 5.0 and hardened through Android 8-17 under NIAP Common Criteria `MDFPP` protection profiles [122, 125]) required satisfying two adversarial constraints simultaneously. First, *protecting corporate assets from untrusted personal apps and lost or stolen hardware*: an unprivileged malware or adware app installed by the employee inside their personal profile (`User 0`) must be unable to read corporate files, scrape the work clipboard, intercept work notifications, ride over the corporate `Always-On VPN`, or access enterprise client TLS certificates stored in work `Keystore` [122, 124], and if a factory-new corporate phone (`Fully Managed` or `COPE`) is stolen from a warehouse or factory-reset by a rogue insider via `Recovery` mode, the handset must automatically re-bind to corporate management before reaching the home screen (`Zero-Touch Enrollment`) [123]. Second, *protecting employee personal privacy from an overreaching or compromised corporate MDM admin*: on a BYOD `Work Profile` (and within the personal side of a `Corporate-Owned, Personally Enabled` `COPE` device), the enterprise `Device Policy Controller` (`DPC`, such as *Microsoft Intune*, *Omnissa Workspace ONE*, or Google's *Android Management API* agent) must be *architecturally barred by the OS* from viewing installed personal packages, reading personal SMS/photos/location, capturing `SecurityLog` / `NetworkLog` DNS history of personal apps ([Chapter 14](14-play-protect-intrusion-logging.md)), or installing a root CA certificate trusted by personal browsers [105, 122]!

## Architecture & AOSP Implementation

### `UserHandle` Kernel Isolation and Independent `FBE` Profile Keys (`CE_10`)

Instead of bolting fragile userspace SDK wrappers ("MAM app wrapping", which historically hooked `libc` file calls inside individual apps and broke on every OS update) onto a single-user environment, `Android Enterprise` builds `Work Profiles` directly atop the exact same kernel `UID`, `SELinux` `MCS`, and `fscrypt` primitives that isolate applications [121, 122]!

![Figure: Android Enterprise Work Profile dual-UID and separate File-Based Encryption (CE_0 vs. CE_10) isolation across Personal User 0 and Managed User 10.](diagrams/17-work-profile-isolation.svg)

When a `DPC` calls `ACTION_PROVISION_MANAGED_PROFILE`, `UserManagerService` creates a managed secondary user profile, conventionally `userId = 10` (`FLAG_MANAGED_PROFILE`) paired to parent `userId = 0` [121]. Examine how every kernel layer separates `User 0` from `User 10`:

First, *kernel `UID` and `SELinux` `MCS` orthogonality*: if *Chrome* (`appId = 10185`) is installed in both the Personal Profile and the badged Work Profile, `PackageManager` shares the immutable read-only `.apk` pages on `/data/app` (`fs-verity` verified, wasting zero duplicate flash space!), yet `Zygote` spawns Personal Chrome as `UID 10185` (`u0_a185`) with `SELinux` categories `c185,c256,c512,c768` and Work Chrome as `UID 1010185` (`u10_a185`, via $100000 \times 10 + 10185$) with distinct `User 10` `MCS` categories [50, 121]! To the Linux kernel VFS, `/dev/binder`, and `keystore2` (`namespace` keyed by full `UID`), `u0_a185` and `u10_a185` are completely unrelated security principals.

Second, *cryptographically separate `File-Based Encryption` keys (`CE_10` and `DE_10`)* [91, 122]: `vold` and `LockSettingsService` generate a *completely independent 256-bit `SyntheticPassword` and `fscrypt` master key $K_{\text{CE}, 10}$* inside `KeyMint`/`StrongBox` for `/data/user/10`, `/data/misc_ce/10`, and `/data/media/10`! In default *Unified Challenge* mode, $K_{\text{CE}, 10}$ is escrow-tied to `User 0`'s lock screen for convenience, whereas enabling a *Separate Work Challenge* (`ACTION_SET_NEW_PASSWORD` on the managed profile) seals $K_{\text{CE}, 10}$ behind a dedicated corporate passcode/`Weaver` slot + `Class 3` biometric gate. When the employee taps the "Work apps" toggle off (*Quiet Mode* at 6:00 p.m. on Friday), `ActivityManagerService` stops all `u10_*` processes and `vold` *evicts $K_{\text{CE}, 10}$ from kernel/ICE memory* (`FS_IOC_REMOVE_ENCRYPTION_KEY`), putting the Work Profile back into cryptographic `BFU` at rest while the employee continues using their personal phone all weekend [91, 122]! When the employee leaves the company (or loses compliance), the `DPC` invokes `DevicePolicyManager.wipeData(0)` inside `User 10`: `vold` instructs `KeyMint`/`StrongBox` to `deleteKey()` on $K_{\text{CE}, 10}$ and $K_{\text{DE}, 10}$ and unlinks `/data/user/10`, achieving *instantaneous cryptographic erasure of all corporate files, emails, and work `Keystore` keys* without touching a single byte of `User 0`'s personal photos or messages [122]!

### Cross-Profile `Intent`, Clipboard, Network, and Certificate Boundaries

To prevent data leakage across the `User 0` $\leftrightarrow$ `User 10` seam during daily work, `DevicePolicyManagerService` enforces three explicit cross-profile gates [122, 124]. First, under `CrossProfileIntentFilter` and *Connected Work and Personal Apps*, an implicit `Intent` (`ACTION_SEND` share sheet or `ACTION_VIEW` link) fired inside `User 10` resolves *strictly* to handlers inside `User 10` by default. Sharing files or clipboard text (`DISALLOW_CROSS_PROFILE_COPY_PASTE`, enabled by default for Work $\to$ Personal!) is blocked unless explicitly permitted by `DPC` policy, while Android 11+ *Connected Apps* (`CrossProfileApps` SDK) requires *both* the IT admin's explicit package allowlist *and* the end user's explicit runtime consent before a calendar or smart-assistant app can bridge non-sensitive views across profiles [124]. Second, under *Per-Profile Always-On `VPN` and Network Routing*, `netd` routes socket traffic using Linux policy-based routing rules keyed by UID range (`ip rule add from all uidrange 1000000-1099999 lookup tun_work`)! Consequently, an enterprise `Always-On VPN` (`setAlwaysOnVpnPackage` + `lockdown = true` blocking fail-open cleartext if VPN drops) tunnels 100 percent of `User 10` work apps directly into the corporate Zero Trust gateway while `User 0`'s personal Netflix, banking, and Signal traffic routes directly out the local Wi-Fi/5G interface unseen by corporate firewalls [122]. Third, under *Scoped CA and mTLS `KeyChain` Certificates*, when a `DPC` installs an internal enterprise Root CA (`installCaCert`) inside a Work Profile, that CA enters *only* `User 10`'s trust store (`/data/misc/user/10/cacerts-added`), so the employer can never MITM `User 0`'s personal TLS sessions! Simultaneously, corporate client authentication keys generated inside `KeyMint`/`StrongBox` (`DevicePolicyManager.generateKeyPair` with *ID Attestation*, verifying hardware-fused `IMEI`/`Serial` inside OID `.17` tags `710..717` exclusively on enterprise-authorized provisioning flows!) authenticate corporate Wi-Fi (`802.1X EAP-TLS`) and Zero Trust Conditional Access without exportability [110, 122].

### Deployment Spectrum (`BYOD`, `COPE`, `Fully Managed`, `COSU`) and `Zero-Touch Enrollment`

Android Enterprise standardizes four management modes scaling from personal ownership to locked-down industrial kiosks [122, 123]:

![Figure: Android Enterprise deployment matrix comparing BYOD Work Profile, COPE, Fully Managed, and Dedicated COSU modes.](diagrams/17-enterprise-deployment-modes.svg)

On corporate-purchased hardware (`COPE`, introduced in Android 8/11 where Android 11+ *removed the old "Work Profile on Fully Managed Device" over-reach* so even on a company-paid `COPE` phone, IT *still* cannot inspect `User 0`'s personal app list or personal files, retaining only device-wide security floors like password complexity, OS update windows, USB disable, and lost-phone factory reset, alongside `Fully Managed` work-only phones and `COSU` `LockTask` POS/airline kiosks), how does an enterprise stop a thief or employee from wiping the phone in `fastboot`/`Recovery` to get an unmanaged phone [122, 123]?

Through `Zero-Touch Enrollment` (and Samsung `Knox Mobile Enrollment`) [34, 123]! Authorized corporate resellers register purchased hardware identifiers + hardware attestation identities directly into Google's Zero-Touch portal before shipping shrink-wrapped boxes to employees. On first boot, and *on every subsequent factory reset*, Android's `SetupWizard` completes network bring-up before unlocking the home screen, queries the Zero-Touch provisioning service over pinned TLS using hardware-attested device identity, downloads the enterprise's `DPC` configuration, verifies `Play Integrity` / `Key Attestation` boot state, and locks the handset directly into `Fully Managed` or `COPE` supervision before `User 0` ever reaches a launcher [123]!

## Vendor, OEM, & Custom-OS Boundaries

Through the *Android Enterprise Recommended (`AER`)* program and NIAP Common Criteria `MDFPP` certifications [125], Google enforces baseline monthly/90-day security patch SLAs, `Zero-Touch` support, and `StrongBox`/`KeyMint` parity across certified enterprise OEMs. Samsung extends standard AOSP `DevicePolicyManager` with `Samsung Knox Platform for Enterprise` (`KPE`) [34], adding hardware-fused `Knox Warranty Bit` tamper attestation (`0x0` genuine vs. `0x1` permanently tripped if `ORANGE` ever booted), `DualDAR` double-layer `BFU` encryption for classified government workloads [125], and granular hardware peripheral/APN controls. Meanwhile, individual high-security users on `GrapheneOS` (`YELLOW`) routinely repurpose Android's native multi-user (`up to 32 users`!) and `Work Profile` isolation locally, running untrusted region-specific apps or Sandboxed Google Play inside a dedicated secondary profile whose `CE_u` encryption keys are evicted completely ("End Session") the second they finish using them [16]!

### When Cross-App Delegation Meets Generative AI

Look closely at how `Android Enterprise` preserved security across applications: *explicit, typed `CrossProfileIntentFilter` / `Connected Apps` contracts instead of ambient cross-sandbox scraping*. In 2025-2026, mobile computing hit an even sharper cross-app boundary test: *on-device Agentic AI assistants* asked by users to summarize private notifications across ten messaging apps, search flight confirmations, and place restaurant or rideshare orders across third-party apps! ## Inspecting via ADB, Fastboot, & Attestation

List all active users and managed profiles (`UserInfo{0:Owner:...}` vs. `UserInfo{10:Work profile:... MANAGED_PROFILE}`), along with active Device Owner / Profile Owner `DPC` component bindings:

```bash
adb shell pm list users
adb shell dumpsys device_policy | grep -E 'Device Owner:|Profile Owner' -A 3
```

Inspect active UID-range policy routing rules in `netd` / `ip rule` when a Work Profile VPN is active, and verify cross-profile `Intent` restrictions on `User 10`:

```bash
adb shell ip rule show | head -n 10
adb shell pm list packages --user 10 | head -n 8
```
