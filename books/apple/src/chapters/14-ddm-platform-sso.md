# Automated Enrollment, Declarative Device Management, and Platform SSO

## Overview

* **Link in the Chain:** Organizational governance and enterprise identity-binding capstone of Part III; couples hardware activation ([Chapter 11](11-keychain-stolen-device.md)) and `SEP` `ACME` attestation ([Chapter 12](12-sep-app-device-attestation.md)) to non-removable fleet supervision (`ADE`), cryptographic BYOD volume isolation (`User Enrollment`), autonomous client state reconciliation (`DDM`), and hardware-bound corporate single sign-on (`Platform SSO`).
* **Core Security Invariant:** Hardware serials assigned in `Apple Business Manager` (`ABM`) cannot complete post-wipe Setup Assistant activation without enrolling into their designated `MDM`/`DDM` server (`IsSupervised = true`), BYOD `User Enrollment` isolates corporate data inside a separate `SEP`-wrapped `APFS` volume, `remotemanagementd` evaluates `DDM` predicates autonomously on-device, and `Platform SSO` binds IdP refresh credentials (`PRT` / OIDC) to non-exportable `SEP` keys.
* **Platform Matrix:** `ABM`/`ASM`, `ADE`, supervision, `User Enrollment`, and `DDM` span `iOS`, `iPadOS`, `macOS`, and `visionOS` (`tvOS` supports `ADE`/`DDM`; `watchOS` supervises via paired `iPhone`), while `Bootstrap Token`, `SetRecoveryLock`, `FileVaultPolicy`, and `LoginWindow` `Platform SSO` target `macOS` (paired with `Extensible Enterprise SSO` on `iOS`/`iPadOS`/`visionOS` and per-user `APFS` quotas on `Shared iPad`).
* **Trusted Computing Base (TCB):** `Apple Business Manager` and activation servers (`albert.apple.com`), `Setup Assistant` (`mbsetupagent`), `mdmclient` and `remotemanagementd` (`DDM` engine), `SEP` `Bootstrap Token` and `Recovery Lock` slots, `ASAuthorizationProviderExtensionLoginManager`, and the enterprise `MDM`/`IdP` control plane.
* **Failure Horizon & Handoff:** Compromising the enterprise `MDM` server or `ABM` tenant grants fleet-wide `PPPC` `TCC` bypasses, `EraseDevice`, and root `pkg` execution on supervised Macs, while downstream web cookies minted after `Platform SSO` remain vulnerable to user-space browser stealers without `DBSC`; hands off zero-knowledge cloud vaults, post-quantum messaging, and confidential AI compute to Part IV ([Chapter 15](15-icloud-hsm-adp.md) through [Chapter 19](19-private-cloud-compute.md)).

## Threat Model & Design Tension

Managing an enterprise fleet of 100,000 Apple Silicon Macs, iPhones, and Vision Pros exposes three structural tensions where traditional workstation agents and legacy mobile profiles fail [51, 52, 395]. First is *post-wipe persistence*: if an insider threat or laptop thief places an un-supervised MacBook into `DFU` mode ([Chapter 1](01-secure-boot.md)) and flashes a clean `Image4`-signed OS from Apple's CDN, every user-space EDR daemon, firewall rule, and configuration profile stored on the wiped `APFS` Data volume evaporates, dropping the attacker into an unmonitored local `admin` account [395, 396]. Conversely, when employees access corporate Slack or email from personal Bring-Your-Own-Device (`BYOD`) phones and Macs, full corporate supervision is unacceptable: employees rightly refuse to grant IT administrators the technical capability to factory-wipe family photographs, inspect personal installed app lists, or intercept personal web traffic through a full-tunnel corporate VPN [397, 398].

Second is *fleet state-reconciliation latency* [51, 399]. Designed in 2010 for small iPhone deployments, imperative `Mobile Device Management` (`MDM`) treats the device as a passive command executor: when a zero-day patch (`iOS 26.3` or `macOS Tahoe 26.3`) ships, the server must send an empty `APNs` wake notification, wait for devices to check in, issue `DeviceInformation` XML plist queries, compute per-device diffs centrally, and enqueue sequential `InstallProfile` or `ScheduleOSUpdate` commands [51, 399]. Across six-figure fleets, that chattiness creates server thundering herds and leaves roaming laptops drifting out of compliance while offline [51, 399].

Third is *bearer-token exfiltration* on cloud-joined workstations [52, 400]. Replacing legacy Active Directory domain binds with OAuth/OIDC cloud identity (`Microsoft Entra ID`, `Okta`, `Ping`) eliminates Kerberos network exposure on coffee-shop Wi-Fi, yet storing unencrypted Primary Refresh Tokens (`PRT`s) or refresh tokens inside user-space files (`~/Library/`) invites *Pass-the-PRT* attacks where commodity malware copies the token off-host and replays corporate sessions from an attacker's VPS [52, 400, 401]. Resolving all three tensions requires supply-chain activation locks (`ADE`) paired with cryptographic BYOD `APFS` partitioning (`User Enrollment`), on-device desired-state evaluation (`DDM`), and `SEP`-bound identity brokers (`Platform SSO`) [51, 52, 395, 397].

## Silicon & System Mechanics

### ABM, ADE Supervision, SEP Escrow, and BYOD Dual-APFS User Enrollment

Enterprise custody begins before an employee breaks the factory seal [395, 402]. When an organization purchases hardware through Apple or authorized resellers, device serial numbers are cryptographically assigned inside *Apple Business Manager (`ABM`)* or *Apple School Manager (`ASM`)* to a designated `MDM`/`DDM` server token, while domain federation (`OIDC` and `SCIM` integration with `Microsoft Entra ID`, `Okta`, or `Google Workspace`) links organizational email domains to *Managed Apple Accounts* [395, 402]. Devices purchased ad hoc can be added manually via `Apple Configurator`, subject to a mandatory 30-day provisional grace period displayed prominently in Settings during which the local user can unilaterally leave management and supervision [395, 402].

On initial power-on (and after *every* subsequent `DFU` restore or `Erase All Content and Settings`), `Setup Assistant` (`mbsetupagent`) contacts Apple's activation service (`albert.apple.com`) over TLS, presenting a hardware signature from `SEP`'s factory-provisioned Silicon Identity Key (`SIK`, [Chapter 12](12-sep-app-device-attestation.md)) before spawning any user shell [340, 348, 395]. Recognizing the serial's `ABM` *Automated Device Enrollment (`ADE`)* record, Apple's Activation CA returns a signed configuration mandate setting `IsSupervised = true`, marking the MDM profile non-removable, and enforcing `AwaitDeviceConfigured` (`AwaitingConfiguration = true` in `information.device.yaml`) [350, 395, 396]. `Setup Assistant` refuses to advance to local desktop creation until `mdmclient` completes `ACME` `Managed Device Attestation`, installs mandatory Zero Trust profiles and `EndpointSecurity` extensions ([Chapter 9](09-esf-xprotect-forensics.md)), and receives an explicit `DeviceConfigured` (`device.configured`) command from the `MDM` server [50, 396]. On `iOS 17+`, `iPadOS 17+`, and `visionOS 2+`, `Return to Service` (`mdm.is-return-to-service`) carries this automation full circle for shared field or clinical hardware: an `EraseDevice` command embeds a bootstrap `Wi-Fi` profile so the wiped unit obliterates its `APFS` Data volume, reconnects to corporate `Wi-Fi` autonomously, fetches its `ADE` profile from `albert.apple.com`, and lands back on the ready home screen with zero human taps [403, 404].

On supervised Apple Silicon Macs, `ADE` locks physical boot and volume cryptography through three `SEP`-resident primitives [7, 123, 403, 405]. First, `SetRecoveryLock` (`passcode.recovery.set`) writes an enterprise-escrowed password directly into `SEP` required to enter One True Recovery (`1TR`) Startup Security Utility, preventing even a local `root` user from downgrading `Full Security` to `Permissive Security` or turning off `SIP` ([Chapter 2](02-mac-localpolicy.md)) [7, 405]. Second, because `APFS` `FileVault` volume encryption requires at least one cryptographically authorized *Volume Owner* (`SecureToken` holder whose `KEK` unwraps the container's Volume Encryption Key inside `SEP`), Apple Silicon Macs automatically generate a hardware-backed *Bootstrap Token* inside `SEP` and escrow it (`BootstrapTokenAllowedForAuthentication`) to the `MDM` server [123, 350]. Whenever a new employee later authenticates at `LoginWindow` via `Platform SSO`, `mdmclient` retrieves the escrowed `Bootstrap Token` to mint a personal `SecureToken` for that user's `OpenDirectory` account and silently authorizes `SSV`/`Cryptex` OS updates without ever creating a shared IT local-admin password [123, 406]. Third, when offboarding or responding to theft, `EraseDevice` checks `EACSPreflight` and triggers `Erase All Content and Settings`: `SEP` destroys the effaceable Data-volume key locker in milliseconds while leaving the sealed `SSV` OS intact for immediate `ADE` re-enrollment (falling back to full `DFU`/Recovery obliteration only if `EACSPreflight` reports an unsealed volume) [350, 403].

![Figure: Account-Driven User Enrollment cryptographic separation on BYOD hardware partitioning personal and enterprise APFS volumes and SEP keybags atop a shared Apple Silicon root.](diagrams/14-extra-byod-dual-apfs-volume-split.svg)

For employee-owned `BYOD` iPhones, iPads, Vision Pros, and Macs, *Account-Driven User Enrollment* (`Settings -> General -> VPN & Device Management -> Sign In to Work or School Account`) replaces intrusive device supervision with mathematical volume separation [397, 398, 407]. When the user enters `alice@corp.example.com`, the OS fetches `https://corp.example.com/.well-known/com.apple.remotemanagement` to discover the enrollment endpoint, opens an isolated `ASWebAuthenticationSession` to the enterprise IdP (`Entra ID` / `Okta`), and receives an `HTTP 308` redirect to `apple-remotemanagement-user-login://authentication-results?access-token=...` [397, 407]. After validating `AssignedManagedAppleID`, `APFS` and `SEP` dynamically mount a *dedicated, cryptographically isolated Enterprise Data `APFS` volume* and separate `secd` Keychain partition wrapped under distinct `SEP` keys [397, 398, 407]:

| Governance Dimension | `ADE` (`ABM` Corporate-Owned) | Account-Driven Device Enrollment | Account-Driven User Enrollment (`BYOD`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supervision (`IsSupervised`) | `true` (mandatory & non-removable) | `false` (removable by local owner) | `false` (strictly user-removable) |
| Storage & Keychain Topology | Full-device management + `Bootstrap Token` | Shared `APFS` volume with managed Separate Data | Dedicated `SEP`-wrapped Enterprise `APFS` volume |
| Wipe Blast Radius | Full `EraseDevice` (`EACS` obliteration) | Full `EraseDevice` permitted if configured | `EraseDevice` forbidden; `Unenroll` shreds Enterprise volume keys only |
| Hardware Serial / `UDID` Visibility | Disclosed & attested (`OIDs 8.9.1`, `8.9.2`) | Disclosed to MDM (`UDID` / `SerialNumber`) | Cryptographically redacted (`OIDs 8.9.1` & `8.9.2` omitted) |
| Network & App Inspection | Device-wide VPN, `ESF`, `PPPC`, full app list | Managed apps & device profiles | Per-App VPN only; personal app list & Photos invisible |

When a BYOD employee leaves the company or taps *Leave Management*, the OS destroys the Enterprise `APFS` volume encryption keys inside `SEP`: every corporate email, managed `Keychain` item, `DDM` asset, and managed app container vanishes instantaneously while personal `Photos`, `Passkeys`, and messages on the personal `APFS` Data volume remain untouched [397, 398].

### Declarative Device Management (DDM): Autonomous State Reconciliation

Specified openly in `apple/device-management/declarative/`, *Declarative Device Management (`DDM`)* (`remotemanagementd` across `iOS 15+`, `macOS 13+`, `tvOS 16+`, `watchOS 10+`, and `visionOS 1.1+`) replaces imperative command-and-poll `MDM` queues with an asynchronous desired-state controller running directly on the endpoint [51, 399, 404, 408, 409].

![Figure: Enterprise fleet management contrasting legacy server-polled imperative MDM commands against ADE-supervised Declarative Device Management (DDM) autonomous local predicate evaluation and proactive StatusChannel streaming.](diagrams/14-ade-ddm-autonomous-loop.svg)

Every `DDM` object inherits from `DeclarationBase` (`declarationbase.yaml`), comprising four canonical fields: `Type` (reverse-DNS schema identifier), `Identifier` ($\le 64\text{-octet}$ UUID), `ServerToken` ($\le 64\text{-octet}$ content-revision digest), and `Payload` [408]. Rather than re-downloading monolithic `.mobileconfig` XML bundles, `remotemanagementd` compares its local `DeclarationsToken` against the server's compact `TokensResponse` (`SyncTokens` in `tokensresponse.yaml`) and fetches *only* declarations whose `ServerToken` changed [409]. Declarations compose into a four-part relational graph [51, 399, 408]: *Assets* (`com.apple.asset.credential.acme` or `useridentity`) define reusable identities; *Configurations* (`com.apple.configuration.softwareupdate.enforcement.specific`, `passcode.settings`, `diskmanagement.settings`, `services.background-tasks`, or `extensible-sso`) specify atomic system settings; *Activations* (`com.apple.activation.simple`) bind sets of Configurations to boolean *Predicate* expressions (`@status(device.operating-system.family) == 'macOS' && @status(passcode.is-compliant) == true`); and *Management* declarations subscribe to telemetry channels [51, 408].

Crucially, `remotemanagementd` evaluates `Activation` predicates *locally and continuously* without network round-trips, resolving multi-activation overlaps deterministically (for example, enforcing the strictest minimum passcode length across all active `passcode.settings` configurations while bridging unmigrated `.mobileconfig` payloads via `com.apple.configuration.legacy`) [51, 399, 408]. On `macOS`, `com.apple.configuration.services.background-tasks` closes a perennial post-exploitation persistence gap: rather than dropping mutable `.plist` files into `/Library/LaunchDaemons` where a local administrator could run `sudo launchctl bootout` to blind an EDR sensor, `DDM` registers tamper-protected `launchd` background services locked against local user disablement in System Settings [51, 404]. When a patch deadline in `softwareupdate.enforcement.specific` approaches, the OS autonomously stages the update, displays escalating user countdown notifications, and enforces installation at the target UTC timestamp even if the laptop is disconnected on an airplane; if a declaration fails deserialization, asset signature verification, or predicate parsing, `remotemanagementd` emits granular `StatusReason` diagnostics (`Error.AssetCannotBeVerified`, `Error.MissingAssets`, `Error.UnableToEvaluatePredicate`, `Info.Predicate`) [399, 408]. Simultaneously, over the *StatusChannel* (`statusreport.yaml`), the client pushes compact JSON `StatusItems` diffs (`softwareupdate.install-state`, `security.certificate.list`, `security.lockdown-mode`, `services.background-task`, `mdm.is-awaiting-configuration`) immediately upon state transition, backed by a 24-hour `FullReport = true` safety-sync snapshot so fleet dashboards stay accurate in real time without polling storms [404].

### Platform Single Sign-On (Platform SSO) on macOS

Introduced in `macOS Ventura 13` (`Platform SSO 1.0`), expanded with Just-In-Time account creation in `Sonoma 14` (`2.0`) and `FileVault` unlock policies in `Sequoia 15`, and extended with zero-touch Setup Assistant registration and NFC `AccessKey` login in `macOS Tahoe 26+` (`com.apple.extensiblesso.yaml` and `extensible-sso.yaml`), *Platform Single Sign-On* anchors cloud Identity Provider (`IdP`) sessions directly inside the Mac's Secure Enclave [52, 400, 406, 410, 411].

![Figure: macOS Platform SSO registration and login sequence binding SEP non-exportable Device Signing and Encryption keys to Enterprise IdP PRT/OIDC assertions and Kerberos TGT mapping.](diagrams/14-platform-sso-sep-binding.svg)

Configured via `ASAuthorizationProviderExtensionLoginManager` (`AuthenticationServices.framework`) inside a vendor's signed SSO System/App Extension (such as Microsoft Company Portal's `Entra ID` Enterprise SSO plug-in or `Okta Verify` FastPass), `Platform SSO` provisions non-exportable `P-256` *Device Signing* and *Device Encryption* (`ECDH`) keys inside `SEP` (`UseSharedDeviceKeys = true` on the system channel so multi-user Macs share one corporate hardware identity) [52, 406, 410, 412]. During silent registration (`RegistrationToken` from `DDM`), the extension calls `attestKey(ofType:clientDataHash:)` (`AllowDeviceIdentifiersInAttestation = true` on corporate Macs) to prove to `Entra ID` or `Okta` via `Apple Enterprise Attestation Root CA` ([Chapter 12](12-sep-app-device-attestation.md)) that both device keys live inside genuine `SEP` silicon, supporting hitless key rotation via `beginKeyRotation` and `completeKeyRotation` [50, 406, 410].

`Platform SSO` supports four distinct `AuthenticationMethod` modes governing how the human user authenticates at `FileVault` pre-boot unlock and `LoginWindow` [52, 406, 411, 412]. First, `UserSecureEnclaveKey` (the gold-standard passwordless posture) generates a *second* per-user non-exportable `SEP` `P-256` signing key unlocked locally by `Touch ID` or the local Mac unlock credential: no corporate password ever touches the Mac or crosses the network, eliminating password-sync drift and phishing simultaneously [52, 406, 412]. Second, `SmartCard` authenticates via hardware PIV tokens (`CryptoTokenKit`). Third, `Password` validates the user's cloud IdP password (`LoginFrequency` defaulting to `64,800 s` = 18 hours, minimum `3,600 s`) and synchronizes it atomically with the local `OpenDirectory` record and `FileVault` `SecureToken` keybag [406]. Fourth, `macOS Tahoe 26+` adds `AccessKey` (`AccessKeyTerminalIdentityUUID` backed by an attested `com.apple.security.acme` terminal key and elliptic-curve `Reader CA Public Key` `AccessKeyReaderIssuerCertificateUUID`) alongside `TemporarySessionQuickLogin` (*Authenticated Guest Mode*), enabling clinicians, airline gate agents, or retail staff to tap an enterprise NFC/Wallet badge to spin up an isolated session [406]. Under `TemporarySessionQuickLogin`, `macOS` balances sub-second shift handoffs against data hygiene by erasing sensitive credential, browser, and Desktop paths inside the temporary home folder immediately after each logout while executing a complete home-directory purge once every eight hours [406].

During sign-in, `Platform SSO` constructs an `RFC 7523` JWT assertion signed inside `SEP` and exchanges it with the IdP for a Primary Refresh Token (`PRT`) whose symmetric session key $K_{\text{session}}$ is wrapped (`JWE` `ECDH-ES`) directly to `SEP`'s non-exportable *Device Encryption Key* [52, 400, 401, 413]:

$$\text{PoP}_{\text{req}} = \text{Sign}_{SK_{\text{SEP}}}\bigl(\text{Header} \,\|\, \{\texttt{aud}, \texttt{iat}, \texttt{nonce}_{\text{IdP}}, H(\text{HTTP\_Req})\}\bigr), \qquad K_{\text{session}} = \text{Decaps}_{SK_{\text{SEP-Enc}}}(\texttt{PRT.encKey})$$

Whenever `Safari`, `Chrome` (via broker plug-ins), native apps, or `com.apple.extensiblesso(kerberos)` (which automatically exchanges/maps cloud identity into on-premises Active Directory `Kerberos` `TGT`s for legacy SMB/internal web shares without AD domain bind [414]) request downstream tokens, `SEP` computes `RFC 9449`-class Proof-of-Possession (`PoP`) signatures over the IdP's fresh nonce [400, 401, 414]. An infostealer exfiltrating `~/Library/` broker SQLite caches to an external server obtains only ciphertext wrapped to `SEP` silicon it cannot call [52, 400].

Furthermore, `Sequoia 15+` and `Tahoe 26+` enforce fine-grained `FileVaultPolicy`, `LoginPolicy`, and `Authorization` governance right inside `com.apple.extensiblesso` (`extensible-sso.yaml`) [406, 411]. Setting `EnableCreateFirstUserDuringSetup = true` (paired with `EnableRegistrationDuringSetup = true` and `com.apple.psso.required` in `macOS Tahoe 26+`) eliminates staging accounts during warehouse-to-employee drop-shipping: the employee authenticates directly against `Entra ID` or `Okta` inside `Setup Assistant`, and `Platform SSO` provisions their local `OpenDirectory` account on the spot [406]. Through `NewUserAuthorizationMode` and `UserAuthorizationMode` (`Standard`, `Admin`, `Groups`, or `Temporary`), `Platform SSO` maps live OIDC/SAML group claims into `AdministratorGroups` and `AuthorizationGroups`, dynamically binding macOS `authd` (`SecurityAgent`) privilege-escalation prompts to cloud group membership rather than static local `admin` flags that linger after role changes [406]. Equally subtle is the anti-downgrade state machine inside `FileVaultPolicy` and `LoginPolicy`: when `RequireAuthentication` is combined with `AllowOfflineGracePeriod`, a traveling user on an airplane can unlock `FileVault` offline within `OfflineGracePeriod`; however, if the Mac *is* online and an attacker or terminated user submits an invalid or revoked cloud credential, `Platform SSO` immediately latches a mandatory online-reauthentication flag that *cannot* be bypassed by unplugging Ethernet or disabling `Wi-Fi` to force an offline fallback [406]!

## Platform Forks Across Apple OSes

Multi-user session architecture drives the sharp split between `macOS` `Platform SSO` and mobile/spatial `Extensible Enterprise SSO` (`ESSO`) [52, 406, 415]. Because `iOS`, `iPadOS`, and `visionOS` run single-owner `SEP` keybags without `OpenDirectory` local UNIX user creation or `FileVault` pre-boot account pickers, they omit the `PlatformSSO` sub-dictionary and instead run `com.apple.extensiblesso` (`Redirect` or `Credential` mode, introduced in `iOS 13+` and `visionOS 1.1+`) where `Microsoft Authenticator` or `Okta Verify` holds the `SEP`-bound `PRT`/device key and brokers Conditional Access across all managed apps (`ScreenLockedBehavior = Cancel` blocking background auth while locked, and `DeniedBundleIdentifiers` fencing off untrusted apps) [400, 406]. For multi-worker shift tablets in healthcare, aviation, and education, `Shared iPad` (`IsMultiUser = true`, `MaximumResidentUsers = 32` in `information.device.yaml`) bridges both paradigms by dynamically carving per-user `APFS` quota containers (`QuotaSize` and `EstimatedResidentUsers`) and `musr`-isolated `secd` Keychain partitions unlocked by federated `Managed Apple Accounts` (`OnlineAuthenticationGracePeriod` for cached shift sign-in, or stateless `TemporarySessionOnly` guest mode that crypto-shreds the session container immediately upon sign-out) [322, 350, 415]. Meanwhile, `visionOS` (`1.1+` / `2.0+`) supports full `ABM` `ADE` supervision, `Return to Service` spatial headset reset between enterprise training users, and `Account-Driven User Enrollment` dual-`APFS` volume separation, whereas `watchOS 10+` inherits organizational supervision and `DDM` declarations (`com.apple.security.acme`, Wi-Fi, VPN) only when paired to a supervised corporate `iPhone` [356, 404, 408].

### The MDM Control Plane as Fleet Ring 0 and the Browser Cookie Horizon

Three operational boundaries define the security perimeter of managed Apple fleets [395, 396, 400, 416, 417]. First, Setup Assistant enrollment security depends critically on `AwaitDeviceConfigured` (`device.configured.yaml`) and mandatory network connectivity in the `ABM` pre-stage profile [395, 396]. Prior to mandatory internet gating in `Setup Assistant`, a user unboxing a factory-sealed laptop could select *"My computer does not connect to the internet,"* create an unmanaged local `UID 501` administrator account, and append `0.0.0.0 iprofiles.apple.com deviceenrollment.apple.com` to `/etc/hosts` before ever enabling Wi-Fi; modern `ABM` profiles forbid offline setup bypass, while the 30-day provisional leave window on manual `Apple Configurator` additions intentionally protects consumers from rogue repair shops or domestic abusers silently locking personal phones into irreversible enterprise supervision [395, 396, 402].

Second, while `SIP` and `TCC` restrict local `root` on an individual Mac ([Chapter 2](02-mac-localpolicy.md), [Chapter 8](08-macos-tcc-sysext.md)), a supervised `macOS` endpoint explicitly delegates sovereign authority to its enrolled `MDM`/`DDM` server [395, 416].

> **Warning.**
> An enterprise `MDM`/`DDM` server managing supervised Macs sits at *Ring 0 across the entire corporate fleet*: it holds escrowed `Bootstrap Token` and `Recovery Lock` secrets, silences `TCC` prompts via `PrivacyPreferencesPolicyControl` (`PPPC` `Full Disk Access`), installs arbitrary `root` packages, and executes instant `EraseDevice` obliteration [123, 395, 403, 405, 416]. Treating the `MDM` server or `ABM` federated IdP tenant with anything less than Tier-0 Active Directory Domain Controller isolation turns fleet management into a single-click ransomware deployment bus [416].

Third, `Platform SSO` solves *Pass-the-PRT* at the identity-broker root without automatically solving *Pass-the-Cookie* inside downstream web applications [400, 417]. Once `Entra ID` or `Okta` verifies the Mac's `SEP` `PoP` signature and redirects Safari or Chrome to a third-party SaaS application via SAML/OIDC, that SaaS backend typically sets a conventional bearer `Set-Cookie` token in the browser's cookie jar (`Chrome Safe Storage`, [Chapter 11](11-keychain-stolen-device.md)). Closing that post-authentication gap requires pairing `Platform SSO` with `W3C` *Device Bound Session Credentials (`DBSC`)* (binding browser cookies themselves to `SEP`/enclave keys), short-lived Continuous Access Evaluation (`CAE`) tokens, and `EndpointSecurity` behavioral rules (`XProtect` `Bastion`, [Chapter 9](09-esf-xprotect-forensics.md)) blocking unauthorized processes from reading browser profile directories [273, 400, 417].

```bash
profiles status -type enrollment
profiles show -type bootstraptoken 2>/dev/null || true
```

Expected read-only output on a supervised corporate Mac:

```text
Enrolled via DEP: Yes
MDM enrollment: Yes (User Approved)
MDM server: https://mdm.corp.example.com/mdm
```

Inspect active `Platform SSO` device/user registration state, `AuthenticationMethod` (`UserSecureEnclaveKey` / `Password`), and `SEP` key binding using `app-sso` [52, 406, 410]:

```bash
app-sso platform -s 2>/dev/null | head -n 18 || true
```

Observe `Declarative Device Management` (`remotemanagementd`) local `Activation` predicate evaluations, `SyncTokens` diffs, and `StatusReport` transmissions in Unified Logging [274, 404, 408]:

```bash
log show --last 1h --predicate 'subsystem == "com.apple.remotemanagementd"' --style compact | tail -n 5
```

Audit `Platform SSO` (`AppSSO` / `AuthenticationServices` extension broker) and built-in `Kerberos` SSO TGT mapping logs [410, 414]:

```bash
log show --last 30m --predicate 'subsystem IN {"com.apple.AppSSO", "com.apple.KerberosExtension"}' --style compact | tail -n 5
```
