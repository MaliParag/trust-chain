# UI-Layer Bypasses, Banking Trojans, and Advanced Protection Lockdown

## Overview

* **Link in the Chain:** Human-interface capability mediation and high-risk system-lockdown layer; governs cross-app UI observation/injection APIs (`AccessibilityService`, `NotificationListenerService`, `SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW`) and unifies platform exploit/scam mitigations under `Advanced Protection Mode`.
* **Core Security Invariant:** Assistive UI-automation capabilities are denied to sideloaded packages by `Enhanced Confirmation Mode` (`Restricted Settings`), sensitive one-time password (`OTP`) notifications and screen contents are redacted from untrusted listeners and screen shares, and enabling `Advanced Protection Mode` atomically locks sideloading, unverified Accessibility, `Arm MTE`, 2G cellular, USB data while locked, and Chrome `JIT`/`WebGPU`.
* **Ecosystem Enforcement Tier:** `ECM` Restricted Settings, `SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW` tapjacking blocks, and `OTP` notification redaction enforce across `GREEN` and `YELLOW` locked states (`AOSP` Android 13-17); `Advanced Protection Mode` (`AdvancedProtectionManager` in Android 16/17) coordinates OS + Play + Chrome policies on `GREEN` certified handsets while `GrapheneOS` (`YELLOW`) exposes equivalent or stricter hardware/exploit toggles natively (`MTE`, USB-C off, Vanadium JIT off, exploit protection compatibility).
* **Trusted Computing Base (TCB):** `AccessibilityManagerService`, `NotificationManagerService` (`SensitiveContentProtectionManager` + `NAS` ML classifier), `WindowManagerService` (`InputDispatcher` touch-obscured flags), `EnhancedConfirmationManager` (`ECM`), and `AdvancedProtectionService` (`frameworks/base/services/core/java/com/android/server/security/advancedprotection/`).
* **Seam & Handoff:** Closes the massive social-engineering loophole where banking trojans hijacked authenticated sessions without breaking `SELinux` or `KeyMint`, while leaving physical at-rest data custody, lock-screen USB attacks, and snatch-and-run street theft to [Chapter 12](12-fbe-usb-theft-protection.md).

## Threat Model & Ecosystem Constraint

Every operating system faces an inescapable tension between *application isolation* and *assistive accessibility*. A blind user relying on a braille display or screen reader (`TalkBack`), or a user with motor impairments relying on voice/switch control (`Switch Access`), requires an assistive service capable of reading the text of *every* visible UI widget across *every* running app (including banking balances and password dialogs) and synthesizing taps, scrolls, and text input on the user's behalf [85].

Criminal Android malware authors realized by 2017 that `android.accessibilityservice.AccessibilityService` is architecturally an *omniscient cross-UID capability* sitting above `Binder`, `SELinux`, `Scoped Storage`, and `KeyMint` [86, 87]! Why spend USD 2,000,000 buying a zero-day `Mali` GPU + `Chrome` exploit chain when a USD 3,000 Crimeware-as-a-Service banking trojan (*Cerberus*, *FluBot*, *SharkBot*, *Xenomorph*, *Hook*, *Crocodilus*) can lure a victim via SMS ("Track your missed DHL parcel") into sideloading an APK and tapping `"Enable Accessibility for Package Tracker"` [86]?

Once a victim granted `BIND_ACCESSIBILITY_SERVICE` (often paired with `BIND_NOTIFICATION_LISTENER_SERVICE` or `SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW` overlays), earlier Android versions lost nearly every practical guarantee on that handset [86, 87]:

First, in the classic *Cloak and Dagger* attack demonstrated by Fratantonio et al. [87], an app holding `SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW` (`draw over other apps`) drew deceptive pixel-aligned overlays over Android's own `Settings` screen ("Tap 'Got It' to win") while routing the user's real finger tap (`tapjacking`) onto the underlying `"Allow"` button of the `AccessibilityService` grant dialog!

Second, once active, the trojan's *Automatic Transfer System* (`ATS`) waited silently until the legitimate user opened their real banking app and passed `BiometricPrompt` + `KeyMint` authentication ([Chapter 13](13-biometrics-passkeys.md)), then dimmed the screen or drew a fake `"Updating Security..."` black overlay, scraped the authenticated `AccessibilityNodeInfo` view hierarchy, intercepted the bank's SMS or push 2FA `OTP` via `NotificationListenerService`, filled in a mule IBAN, and called `AccessibilityNodeInfo.performAction(ACTION_CLICK)` / `dispatchGesture()` to wire out funds *from inside the genuine, attested banking app on the victim's genuine `GREEN` locked handset* [86]! Even remote `Play Integrity` (`MEETS_STRONG_INTEGRITY`, [Chapter 16](16-play-integrity-keybox-wars.md)) passed 100 percent because the bootloader was locked and the kernel was untouched.

Simultaneously, at the opposite end of the threat spectrum, journalists, dissidents, diplomats, and executives faced targeted commercial spyware (*Pegasus*, *Predator*, [Chapter 20](20-multivendor-spyware-chains.md)) exploiting browser `V8` JIT compilers, `WebGPU` shaders, 2G cellular basebands, and physical USB ports: settings previously scattered across fifteen obscure sub-menus across `Settings`, `Chrome://flags`, and `Play Protect` that no human under stress could configure reliably without missing a toggle [5, 89].

## Architecture & AOSP Implementation

### Defending the UI Loop: Touch-Obscured Input, `ECM` Restricted Settings, and `OTP` Redaction

Android closed the UI-layer confused-deputy surface across four progressive structural gates in `WindowManagerService`, `EnhancedConfirmationManager`, and `NotificationManagerService` [85, 88, 90]:

First, *eliminating overlay tapjacking (`HIDE_NON_SYSTEM_OVERLAY_WINDOWS` & untrusted touch blocking)*: Android 10 through Android 12 overhauled `InputDispatcher` inside `WindowManagerService`. All security-critical system dialogs (`PackageInstaller`, `PermissionController` runtime grants, `BiometricPrompt`, `Accessibility` enablement, `DeviceAdmin`, VPN confirmation) automatically carry `SYSTEM_FLAG_HIDE_NON_SYSTEM_OVERLAY_WINDOWS`, evicting third-party `TYPE_APPLICATION_OVERLAY` windows while the prompt is visible. Furthermore, Android 12 blocked *all* non-system overlays from passing touches through (`FLAG_NOT_TOUCHABLE` pass-through attacks) unless window alpha $\le 0.8$ or explicitly trusted, setting `MotionEvent.FLAG_WINDOW_IS_OBSCURED` / `FLAG_WINDOW_IS_PARTIALLY_OBSCURED` and dropping untrusted obscured touches outright (`filterTouchesWhenObscured="true"`) [85].

![Figure: Banking-trojan UI-layer kill chain over AccessibilityService and platform blocks via Restricted Settings (ECM) and Sensitive OTP Redaction.](diagrams/11-accessibility-trojan-killchain.svg)

Second, `Enhanced Confirmation Mode` (`ECM` / *Restricted Settings*) in Android 13 (`API 33`), expanded into `EnhancedConfirmationManager` in Android 15 [88], distinguishes a genuine screen reader installed from an app store using Android's `Session-based PackageInstaller` (`INSTALL_SOURCE` verified) from a phishing APK downloaded via Chrome, WhatsApp, or a file manager (`local file` / direct URI install). When an APK installed outside trusted session-store flows declares `<service android:permission="android.permission.BIND_ACCESSIBILITY_SERVICE">` or `BIND_NOTIFICATION_LISTENER_SERVICE`, `ECM` marks those capabilities *Restricted* (`AppOpsManager.MODE_ERRORED`). When the social-engineered user follows the scammer's instructions into `Settings` $\to$ `Accessibility`, the toggle is greyed out and unclickable ("Restricted setting: For your security, this setting is currently unavailable") unless the user navigates a multi-step out-of-band confirmation path gated by `LSKF` authentication (and blocked *completely* without override when `Advanced Protection` or live call-scam protection is active!) [88, 89]. Android 14+ further added `android:isAccessibilityTool="true"` metadata audits and visual privacy indicators warning users whenever non-assistive apps hold active screen observation.

Third, *Sensitive Window & One-Time Password (`OTP`) Redaction* in Android 14 through Android 16/17 protects visible and notified secrets across three channels [85, 90]. Apps or password fields setting `View.setAccessibilityDataSensitive(ACCESSIBILITY_DATA_SENSITIVE_YES)` restrict `AccessibilityNodeInfo` inspection and `performAction()` strictly to verified assistive accessibility tools (`isAccessibilityTool`), hiding sensitive balances and credential views from generic automation services [85]. During screen sharing (`MediaProjection`) or remote-access scams ("Install AnyDesk/TeamViewer so bank support can help you"), Android 15+ automatically blanks sensitive notification banners, lock-screen PIN pads, and `FLAG_SECURE` / `setContentSensitivity` views so remote viewers see a redacted placeholder while the local user still sees their screen [90]. Meanwhile, inside `NotificationManagerService` (`NotificationAssistantService` on-device ML/regex classifier), Android 15+ identifies incoming notifications containing 2FA `OTP` codes (from SMS, authenticator pushes, or email) and strips their content from *all* untrusted `NotificationListenerService` apps lacking the privileged `RECEIVE_SENSITIVE_NOTIFICATIONS` role (limiting visibility strictly to the user's Companion watch/wearable manager or verified carrier/system handlers), with Android 16/17 extending `OTP` withholding for up to 3 hours under untrusted/recent-unlock or remote-session anomaly signals [89, 90].

### Android 16/17 `Advanced Protection Mode`: Single-Toggle High-Risk Lockdown

Introduced in Android 16 (`API 36`) and expanded in Android 17 (`android.security.advancedprotection.AdvancedProtectionManager`), `Advanced Protection Mode` solves the high-risk user problem at the operating-system level: a single hardware-authenticated toggle (`Settings` $\to$ `Security & privacy` $\to$ `Advanced Protection`, synced optionally with Google Account Advanced Protection) that atomically switches the entire handset from consumer convenience defaults to maximum exploit, network, physical, and social-engineering resistance, and prevents even the user or MDM-less apps from accidentally turning individual sub-defenses back off while `Advanced Protection` remains active [5, 89]!

![Figure: Android 16/17 Advanced Protection Mode single-toggle enforcement across app installation, memory safety, browser JIT/WebGPU, radio, USB, and third-party apps.](diagrams/11-advanced-protection-lockdown.svg)

Examine the exact cross-layer policies enforced simultaneously when `AdvancedProtectionService` activates [5, 89]. Across the *application and UI-abuse surface*, it blocks *all* unknown-source APK sideloading (`DISALLOW_INSTALL_UNKNOWN_SOURCES_GLOBALLY`, even via pre-granted browser/file-manager installers!), mandates real-time `Google Play Protect` scanning ([Chapter 14](14-play-protect-intrusion-logging.md)), blocks granting `AccessibilityService` or `NotificationListener` to unverified apps, and enables AI-powered *Scam Detection* on calls and messages (actively blocking users from disabling Play Protect, enabling Accessibility, or starting screen sharing *while an active phone call with an unknown number is in progress*!). Across the *memory-corruption and zero-click browser surface*, it turns on hardware `Arm MTE` ([Chapter 8](08-arm-mte-scudo.md)) across all eligible applications, enforces Chrome `HTTPS-First` Mode (blocking cleartext `http://` connections without explicit warning confirmation), and disables Chrome JavaScript `V8` JIT compilation (`JavaScript Optimizer`) and `WebGPU` shaders on untrusted sites, eliminating the single largest remote renderer RCE and speculative side-channel (`TIKTAG`) attack surface on mobile devices [68, 89]. Across the *radio and physical lock surface*, it disables 2G cellular connectivity at the `IRadio` baseband HAL level (preventing false-base-station IMSI-catcher downgrade attacks that exploit unauthenticated GSM ciphers or legacy 2G modem parsers) and prohibits auto-reconnecting to insecure open/WEP Wi-Fi networks; locks the USB-C port to charge-only when the screen is locked ([Chapter 12](12-fbe-usb-theft-protection.md)); activates `Theft Detection Lock`, `Offline Device Lock`, and `Inactivity Reboot` (automatically rebooting the handset back to `BFU` state if left locked and untouched for 72 hours!) [89, 97]; and enables End-to-End Encrypted `Intrusion Logging` ([Chapter 14](14-play-protect-intrusion-logging.md)) [106]. Finally, through the *ecosystem signal API* (`AdvancedProtectionManager`), it exposes a zero-PII boolean query + callback (`isAdvancedProtectionEnabled()`, gated by normal permission `QUERY_ADVANCED_PROTECTION_MODE`) so third-party apps (*Signal*, *WhatsApp*, banking apps, enterprise clients) can automatically harden their own internal threat posture (silencing unknown callers, relaying WebRTC calls through servers to hide IP addresses, disabling automatic media attachment downloads, and requiring passkeys) the instant the OS toggle flips on [89]!

## Vendor, OEM, & Custom-OS Boundaries

Google's `Advanced Protection Mode` builds atop standard AOSP `AdvancedProtectionManager` hooks in Android 16+, paired on certified `GREEN` handsets with Play Protect and Chrome integrations; Samsung mirrors similar protections on Galaxy devices via `Samsung Auto Blocker` (*Maximum Restrictions* mode, which blocks sideloading, blocks USB cable commands, strips message image attachments via isolated Samsung Message Guard sandboxes, disables 2G, and removes location metadata on share) [34, 89].

On `GrapheneOS` (`YELLOW` locked Pixel), every underlying hardware/kernel hardening inside `Advanced Protection` has long been available (and in several cases set to *strict defaults for all users* rather than opt-in!): `GrapheneOS` enables synchronous `Arm MTE` by default ([Chapter 8](08-arm-mte-scudo.md)), disables V8 JIT by default inside its `Vanadium` browser/`WebView`, exposes hardware USB-C port control (`Off`, `Charging-only`, `Charging-only when locked` disabling USB-C controller data lanes at the Type-C PD chip level!), configurable auto-reboot from `10 minutes` to `72 hours` (far tighter than stock Android's 72-hour window!), and per-network/2G cellular locks without requiring any cloud account [16, 96].

### The Permanent Trade-Off of Assistive Capabilities

Even with `ECM` Restricted Settings and `ACCESSIBILITY_DATA_SENSITIVE_YES`, assistive computing embodies an irreducible philosophical boundary: *a genuine motor-control or screen-reading assistive tool legitimately installed from an official store and designated `isAccessibilityTool="true"` must still be able to see and interact with the UI on behalf of a disabled human* [85]. Platform security cannot delete `AccessibilityService` without disenfranchising millions of users; instead, Android quarantines *how* an app can acquire that capability (blocking sideloaded, in-call, and `Advanced Protection` paths), hides transient secrets (`OTP`s), and provides purpose-built least-privilege cross-app APIs (`AppFunctions` in [Chapter 18](18-on-device-agentic-ai.md)) so mainstream automation never needs `AccessibilityService` at all.

Having secured running processes and the interactive UI, what happens when the handset is snatched out of the user's hand while unlocked on a city street, or seized while locked and plugged into a forensic lab's physical USB zero-day rig? [Chapter 12](12-fbe-usb-theft-protection.md) examines `File-Based Encryption`, physical USB exploits, `Identity Check`, and `Theft Detection Lock`.

## Inspecting via ADB, Fastboot, & Attestation

List all currently enabled `AccessibilityService` and `NotificationListenerService` components across the handset (any unexpected third-party package here during an incident investigation is an immediate high-severity indicator!):

```bash
adb shell settings get secure enabled_accessibility_services
adb shell settings get secure enabled_notification_listeners
```

Inspect active `Advanced Protection` / `Enhanced Confirmation` (`ecm`) and 2G / USB security posture via `dumpsys` and `cmd`:

```bash
adb shell dumpsys advanced_protection 2>/dev/null | head -n 12
adb shell appops get --uid 10200 ACCESS_RESTRICTED_SETTINGS 2>/dev/null
```

Audit apps currently granted `SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW` (`draw over other apps`) across the device via `appops`:

```bash
adb shell cmd appops query-op SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW allow
```
