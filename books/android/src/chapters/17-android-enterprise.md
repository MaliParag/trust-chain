# Android Enterprise: Work Profiles, COPE, and Zero-Touch

## Overview

* **Link in the Chain:** Multi-user cryptographic profile compartmentalization and fleet-management layer; isolates corporate applications, networks, SELinux categories, and `fscrypt` keys inside a managed `UserHandle` (`Work Profile`) or full-device `DevicePolicyManager` boundary anchored by hardware-bound `Zero-Touch Enrollment`.
* **Core Security Invariant:** A managed `Work Profile` (`user 10+`) executes every corporate package under an isolated kernel `UID` namespace (`100000 * userId + appId`), disjoint SELinux Multi-Level Security (`MLS`) category sets (`s0:c522,c768` vs. personal `s0:c512,c768`), and an independent `File-Based Encryption` key $K_{\text{CE}, 10}$, allowing an enterprise controller to pause (`FS_IOC_REMOVE_ENCRYPTION_KEY`) or cryptographically shred corporate storage without reading or harming `User 0` personal data.
* **Ecosystem Enforcement Tier:** Multi-user `UserHandle`, `seapp_contexts` `MLS` isolation, separate `fscrypt` profile keys, `CrossProfileIntentFilter`, and `DevicePolicyManager` APIs enforce identically across AOSP `GREEN` and `YELLOW` (`GrapheneOS` users leverage native secondary users, `Private Space`, and `Shelter`/`Insular` Work Profiles locally); cloud `Zero-Touch Enrollment` and `Android Management API` bind to GMS (`GREEN`) fleets alongside Samsung `Knox Mobile Enrollment`.
* **Trusted Computing Base (TCB):** `UserManagerService` (`UserHandle` UID arithmetic), `libselinux` `selinux_android_setcontext` (`c512..c1023` category assignment), `vold` + `LockSettingsService` + `KeyMint` per-user `CE`/`DE` key derivation ([Chapter 12](12-fbe-usb-theft-protection.md)), `DevicePolicyManagerService` and `PackageManagerService` (`CrossProfileIntentResolver`), and the provisioned Device Policy Controller (`DPC`).
* **Seam & Handoff:** Reconciles corporate data loss prevention (`DLP`), per-profile `Always-On VPN` lockdown, `KeyChain` mTLS hardware keys, and instant offboarding wipes with cryptographic employee privacy across `BYOD` and `COPE` fleets; transitions to [Chapter 18](18-on-device-agentic-ai.md) to examine how Android isolates on-device and cross-app Agentic AI (`AICore`, `Private Compute Core`, and `AppFunctions`).

## Threat Model & Ecosystem Constraint

Android's original mobile management surface, the legacy `Device Administrator` API introduced in Android 2.2 (`Froyo`) and deprecated across Android 9 and Android 10, suffered from an architectural all-or-nothing flaw [402, 467]. Enrolling an employee's personal smartphone in corporate email required granting an employer's Mobile Device Management (`MDM`) APK coarse, device-wide authority (including `wipeData(0)` capable of factory-resetting the employee's personal family photographs and `resetPassword()` capable of locking the entire handset), while simultaneously leaving corporate PDF attachments, OAuth refresh tokens, and internal root CA certificates sitting inside the exact same `User 0` filesystem, clipboard, and TLS trust store as the employee's unmanaged personal games and ad-supported utilities [402, 467]. Worse still, banking trojans and ransomware families routinely social-engineered consumers into enabling `DeviceAdminReceiver` solely to block uninstallation and hold lock screens hostage. Employees rightly rejected enrollment out of privacy fear; enterprise security architects rightly distrusted single-namespace BYOD phones.

Attempting to patch that gap in userspace via third-party "Mobile Application Management" (`MAM`) SDK wrappers (which rewrote bytecode or hooked `bionic` `libc` file and socket calls inside repackaged corporate APKs) proved equally brittle, breaking on every `ART` compiler update and collapsing whenever native JNI code invoked raw system calls directly. Engineering a true OS-native enterprise substrate (`Android Enterprise`, introduced in Android 5.0 and hardened through Android 11-17 against NIAP Common Criteria `MDFPP` protection profiles [402, 467]) required satisfying two symmetric, mutually distrusting threat models on a single physical SoC:

First, *defending corporate confidentiality and compliance against untrusted personal apps, network adversaries, and physical device loss*: an adware or spyware APK installed by the employee inside their personal profile (`User 0`) must be blocked by the kernel and framework from reading corporate databases, scraping the work clipboard, resolving `ContentProvider` URIs across profiles, piggybacking onto the corporate `Always-On VPN`, or signing challenges with enterprise client TLS keys in `KeyMint` [402, 468, 469]. Furthermore, if a corporate-purchased handset (`Fully Managed`, `COPE`, or `COSU`) is stolen in transit or wiped via `Recovery` mode by an insider, the handset must deterministically re-enroll into enterprise supervision during `SetupWizard` before reaching a launcher (`Zero-Touch Enrollment`) [470, 471].

Second, *defending employee personal privacy against an overreaching or compromised enterprise administrator*: on a personally owned `BYOD` Work Profile (and even on the personal side of an organization-owned `COPE` handset from Android 11 onward [467]), the OS reference monitor must *architecturally forbid* the enterprise `Device Policy Controller` (`DPC`, such as *Microsoft Intune*, *Omnissa Workspace ONE*, or Google's *Android Management API* agent [472]) from enumerating installed `User 0` packages, reading personal SMS, call logs, photos, or GPS coordinates, capturing personal DNS/TCP `NetworkLog` or `SecurityLog` streams, or injecting a corporate TLS interception CA into `User 0`'s browser trust store [402, 467, 471].

## Architecture & AOSP Implementation

### Multi-User Kernel Separation: `UserHandle` UID Math, SELinux `MLS` Categories, and `fscrypt` Profile Keys

Rather than inventing a parallel container layer, `Android Enterprise` constructs the managed `Work Profile` (`android.os.usertype.profile.MANAGED`, conventionally assigned `userId = 10` or higher and bound as a child profile to primary `USER_SYSTEM = 0` inside `UserManagerService` [473, 474]) atop three orthogonal kernel enforcement primitives: Linux `UID` arithmetic, SELinux Multi-Level Security (`MLS` / `MCS`) category lattices, and independent `fscrypt` File-Based Encryption keys [92, 473, 475, 476].

![Figure: Android Enterprise Work Profile dual-UID and separate File-Based Encryption (CE_0 vs. CE_10) isolation across Personal User 0 and Managed User 10.](diagrams/17-work-profile-isolation.svg)

Examine the exact arithmetic inside `frameworks/base/core/java/android/os/UserHandle.java` [473]. Android partitions the 32-bit Linux `uid_t` space into contiguous blocks of `PER_USER_RANGE = 100000` UIDs per user or profile:

$$\mathrm{UID}(u, a) = u \times 100{,}000 + (a \bmod 100{,}000)$$

where $u \in \{0, 10, 11, \dots\}$ is the `userId` and $10{,}000 \le a \le 19{,}999$ is the base `appId` (`AID_APP_START = 10000`) assigned by `PackageManagerService` at install time [473]. When an employee runs Google Chrome (`appId = 10145`) in both their Personal Profile (`u = 0`) and their badged Work Profile (`u = 10`), `PackageManager` deduplicates the read-only, `fs-verity`-protected base `.apk` and `.odex` pages under `/data/app` so the binary consumes flash storage only once. At `Zygote` fork time, however, Personal Chrome drops privileges to `UID 10145` (`u0_a145`) with private data mounted at `/data/user/0/com.android.chrome`, whereas Work Chrome drops privileges to `UID 1010145` (`u10_a145`) with corporate data mounted at `/data/user/10/com.android.chrome` [473, 474]! To the Linux kernel VFS DAC checks, `/dev/binder` caller-identity stamps (`binder_transaction_data.sender_euid`), Linux `netd` socket marks, and `keystore2`'s UID-keyed SQLite key database, `UID 10145` and `UID 1010145` are completely disjoint principals.

Even if a buggy corporate app accidentally called `chmod 0777` on a sensitive file inside `/data/user/10`, kernel Mandatory Access Control blocks cross-profile reads before VFS permissions are even consulted [475]. Inside `system/sepolicy/private/seapp_contexts` and `libselinux`'s `selinux_android_setcontext()`, `untrusted_app` domains (targeting `API 30+`) enforce `levelFrom=all` (while `platform_app` and `isolated_app` enforce `levelFrom=user`) [475]. Under `libselinux`'s bit-splitting category encoder, the per-app offset $i = a - 10000$ maps onto low categories `c0..c511` as $(c_{i \bmod 256},\, c_{256 + \lfloor i / 256 \rfloor})$, while the `userId` $u$ maps onto high categories `c512..c1023` as:

$$\mathrm{Cat}_{\text{user}}(u) = \bigl(c_{512 + (u \ \& \ \text{0xff})},\; c_{768 + ((u \gg 8) \ \& \ \text{0xff})}\bigr)$$

Substitute $u = 0$ (`USER_SYSTEM`) and $u = 10$ (`Work Profile`) for Chrome ($i = 145$) [475]: Personal Chrome (`UID 10145`) executes in SELinux context `u:r:untrusted_app:s0:c145,c256,c512,c768` (or `s0:c512,c768` for `levelFrom=user` system-signed helpers), whereas Work Chrome (`UID 1010145`) executes in `u:r:untrusted_app:s0:c145,c256,c522,c768` (with files under `/data/user/10` labeled `u:object_r:app_data_file:s0:c145,c256,c522,c768`)! Because AOSP's compiled `mls` policy (`mlsconstrain`) requires category-set dominance/equality between subject and object on `file`, `dir`, `unix_stream_socket`, and `process` (`ptrace`/`sigkill`) classes, `{c512, c768}` and `{c522, c768}` are mathematically incomparable sets, sealing the cross-profile wall inside the kernel LSM [475].

![Figure: Four-layer kernel and framework isolation wall separating Personal User 0 (UID 10145, c512, CE_0) from Managed Work Profile User 10 (UID 1010145, c522, CE_10).](diagrams/17-extra-work-profile-uid-mls-fbe-wall.svg)

Beneath `UID` and `MLS` sits the cryptographic storage anchor: *independent per-profile `fscrypt` `CE` and `DE` encryption hierarchies* (`LockSettingsService`, `SyntheticPasswordManager`, and `system/vold/FsCrypt.cpp`) [92, 476, 477]. When `User 10` is provisioned, `vold` generates fresh random 256-bit Device Encrypted ($K_{\text{DE}, 10}$) and Credential Encrypted ($K_{\text{CE}, 10}$) master keys wrapped independently through `KeyMint`/`StrongBox` for `/data/misc_de/10`, `/data/user_de/10`, `/data/misc_ce/10`, `/data/user/10`, and `/data/media/10` [92, 476]. Under *Unified Lock*, `LockSettingsService` ties $K_{\text{CE}, 10}$'s `SyntheticPassword` escrow token to `User 0`'s lock screen for seamless UX; when enterprise policy requires a *Separate Work Challenge* (`ACTION_SET_NEW_PASSWORD` on `User 10`), $K_{\text{CE}, 10}$ binds to its own dedicated corporate passcode and `Titan M2` `Weaver` slot, remaining cryptographically locked in `BFU` even after the employee unlocks their personal phone [471, 477]. Moreover, because Android 11 completely removed the insecure legacy `resetPassword()` API (which previously could not unwrap `FBE` keys without the old credential), IT helpdesks performing *Secure Passcode Reset* on a forgotten Separate Work Challenge must pre-provision a 256-bit cryptographic escrow secret (`setResetPasswordToken`, activated once while `User 10` is unlocked) so `SyntheticPasswordManager` can derive an alternate `KeyMint`-guarded unwrap path via `resetPasswordWithToken()` during Direct Boot [467, 471, 477].

Trace what happens at the hardware storage layer during two everyday lifecycle events [468, 476, 478]: when the employee toggles "Work apps" off (*Quiet Mode*, `UserManager.requestQuietModeEnabled(true, user10)` [478]) on Friday evening, `ActivityManagerService` halts every `u10_*` process and `vold`'s `fscrypt_lock_user_key(10)` invokes the Linux kernel `FS_IOC_REMOVE_ENCRYPTION_KEY` `ioctl`, zeroizing $K_{\text{CE}, 10}$ from kernel page-cache metadata and reprogramming the SoC's UFS Inline Crypto Engine (`ICE`) keyslots so every corporate file reverts to cold `AES-256-XTS` ciphertext at rest while `User 0` continues running uninterrupted [92, 476, 478]! And when the employee leaves the company (or a compliance check fails), the `DPC` calls `DevicePolicyManager.wipeData(0)` inside `User 10`: `vold` runs `fscrypt_destroy_user_key(10)`, commanding `KeyMint`/`StrongBox` to execute `deleteKey()` on $K_{\text{CE}, 10}$ and $K_{\text{DE}, 10}$ before unlinking `/data/user/10`, cryptographically shredding gigabytes of corporate state in milliseconds while leaving `User 0`'s personal `CE_0` key and data 100 percent untouched [468, 476]. (Android 15+ applies this exact same `UserHandle` + `MLS` + separate `fscrypt` key architecture to consumer *Private Space*, `android.os.usertype.profile.PRIVATE`, which enterprises can govern or block on managed devices via `UserManager.DISALLOW_ADD_PRIVATE_PROFILE` so unmanaged shadow profiles cannot hide data on corporate hardware [474, 479].)

### The Framework Gateway: `CrossProfileIntentFilter`, `ContentProvider` URI Guards, and `Connected Apps`

Because `UID` and `SELinux` block raw filesystem and IPC access between `User 0` and `User 10`, legitimate cross-profile workflows (such as viewing free/busy calendar slots or dialing a corporate contact) must traverse explicit framework reference monitors inside `PackageManagerService` (`CrossProfileIntentResolver`) and `ActivityManagerService` [468, 480, 481].

By default, implicit `Intent` resolution (`ACTION_SEND`, `ACTION_VIEW`, `ACTION_PICK`) is strictly confined to the calling `userId` [480]. Only explicit system-approved bridges registered via `DevicePolicyManager.addCrossProfileIntentFilter()` cross the boundary (`FLAG_PARENT_CAN_ACCESS_MANAGED` or `FLAG_MANAGED_CAN_ACCESS_PARENT`), while `DISALLOW_CROSS_PROFILE_COPY_PASTE` prevents corporate text copied via `ClipboardManager` inside `User 10` from being read by `User 0` input editors, and separate Personal/Work tabs in the Android 11+ Share Sheet, Photo Picker, and `DocumentsUI` enforce policy checks before passing a `ClipData` file descriptor across profiles [467, 468]. Similarly, Android's `ContentProvider` subsystem parses cross-user URI prefixes of the form `content://10@media/external/images/media` (`ContentProvider.getUserIdFromUri()`), which `ActivityManagerService` and `UriGrantsManagerService` gate behind `INTERACT_ACROSS_USERS` or `INTERACT_ACROSS_USERS_FULL` privileged system permissions (`checkCrossUserPermission()`), rejecting any unprivileged `User 0` query targeting a `10@...` authority unless an explicit, path-scoped `FLAG_GRANT_READ_URI_PERMISSION` capability was intentionally delegated across an approved cross-profile intent [480, 482]. Whenever a system app or `APEX` module omits that cross-user check on an exported helper endpoint (as occurred in `MediaProvider` `CVE-2023-40127` [483] and in the `Settings` Slice controller `BluetoothScanningMainSwitchPreferenceController.getAvailabilityStatus()` `CVE-2023-21247`, which let local callers toggle Bluetooth location scanning via a Slice URI despite `DISALLOW_CONFIG_LOCATION` [484]), the bug constitutes an OS-level Elevation of Privilege patched via monthly security bulletins and `Project Mainline` updates. For rich app pairs such as Calendar or smart assistants, Android 11+ introduced the `CrossProfileApps` (`INTERACT_ACROSS_PROFILES`) framework (*Connected Work and Personal Apps*), requiring a dual-key consent ceremony: *both* the IT administrator (`setCrossProfilePackages`) *and* the end user (via a system-rendered Settings toggle) must explicitly authorize a specific signed package pair before cross-profile `Binder` channels can open [481].

### Management Modes (`BYOD`, `COPE`, `Fully Managed`, `COSU`) and `DevicePolicyManager` Controls

Across enterprise fleets, `DevicePolicyManager` (`DPM`) and Google's declarative cloud-driven `Android Management API` (`AMAPI`, which executes via the Play-updated *Android Device Policy* app without requiring vendors to maintain custom Java DPC agents [472]) enforce four canonical management postures [402, 467, 485]:

![Figure: Android Enterprise deployment matrix comparing BYOD Work Profile, COPE, Fully Managed, and Dedicated COSU modes.](diagrams/17-enterprise-deployment-modes.svg)

Compare the exact architectural authority of the `DPC` across those four modes [402, 467, 485]. In *BYOD Work Profile* (`Profile Owner` on a personally owned handset), the `DPC` lives strictly inside `User 10` and governs only `User 10`, modulo minimal device-wide hygiene floors such as requiring a baseline screen lock complexity (`setRequiredPasswordComplexity`) or blocking unknown-source APK sideloading via Play managed configurations (`verify_apps:device_wide_unknown_source_block` + `ENSURE_VERIFY_APPS` [471]). In *Fully Managed* (`Device Owner` on a work-only handset with no personal profile) and *Dedicated Devices* (`COSU` single-app retail POS, logistics scanners, or airline check-in kiosks pinned into `LockTask` mode via `setLockTaskPackages` and `setUserControlDisabledPackages` blocking force-stop or cache wipes [467, 485]), the `DPC` owns `User 0` across the entire device, streaming enterprise `SecurityLog` (`TAG_APP_PROCESS_START`, `TAG_KEYGUARD_DISMISSED`, `TAG_ADB_SHELL_CMD`) and `NetworkLog` DNS/TCP telemetry ([Chapter 14](14-play-protect-intrusion-logging.md) [471]).

Between `BYOD` and `Fully Managed` sits Android's most important privacy-engineering redesign: *Corporate-Owned, Personally Enabled (`COPE`)*, formalized in Android 11 as *Work Profile on Company-Owned Devices* (`isOrganizationOwnedDeviceWithManagedProfile() == true`) [402, 467]. In Android 8 through 10, organizations deploying dual-use company phones used a deprecated hybrid called `COMP` (*Work Profile on a Fully Managed Device*), where a `Device Owner` still sat inside `User 0`, tempting employers to inventory personal apps or inspect personal traffic! Android 11 abolished `COMP` completely [467]: on a modern `COPE` handset provisioned from `SetupWizard`, *there is no `Device Owner` inside `User 0`*; the `DPC` exists solely as `Profile Owner` inside `User 10`, preserving the exact cryptographic `CE_0` and package-visibility privacy wall of `BYOD` for the employee's personal life while unlocking specific hardware asset-protection levers appropriate for company-purchased hardware. Those organization-owned levers include `Common Criteria Mode` (`setCommonCriteriaModeEnabled`, adding `AES-GCM` wrapping to Bluetooth Long-Term Keys and Wi-Fi configuration stores [467]), `FactoryResetProtectionPolicy` (`FRP`), OS system-update freeze and maintenance windows, `DISALLOW_USB_FILE_TRANSFER`, and Android 15-17 Enterprise hardware-hardening policies including `setUsbDataSignalingEnabled(false)` physical USB port lockdown, `DISALLOW_CELLULAR_2G` stingray defense, and mandatory `Arm MTE` / `Advanced Protection` posture enforcement [467, 471, 486].

Three fine-grained `DevicePolicyManager` mechanisms complete runtime enterprise isolation [468, 469, 471, 487]. First, *`DelegatedScopes`* (`setDelegatedScopes`) decomposes monolithic `DPC` super-privileges across specialized least-privilege helper apps inside the profile (`DELEGATION_CERT_INSTALL` for an internal SCEP/EST PKI agent, `DELEGATION_APP_RESTRICTIONS` for a config manager, `DELEGATION_BLOCK_UNINSTALL`, and `DELEGATION_SECURITY_LOGGING` / `DELEGATION_NETWORK_LOGGING` for a dedicated mobile SIEM collector, so compromising or misconfiguring a certificate helper never grants `wipeData()` power [487]). Second, *Per-Profile `Always-On VPN` with `lockdown`* (`setAlwaysOnVpnPackage(admin, vpnPkg, /* lockdownEnabled = */ true)`, which Android 11+ prevents users from toggling off manually [467, 469]) instructs `ConnectivityService` and `netd` to install Linux policy-routing UID rules (`ip rule add from all uidrange 1000000-1099999 lookup tun0`, paired with blackhole fail-closed rules if `tun0` drops), tunneling 100 percent of `User 10` sockets into the corporate Zero Trust SASE gateway while `User 0`'s personal traffic exits local Wi-Fi/5G untouched and unseen [402, 469]. Third, *Scoped Certificates and Hardware ID-Attested `KeyChain` Keys* confine admin-installed enterprise CAs (`installCaCert`) strictly to `/data/misc/user/10/cacerts-added` (never `User 0`'s TLS trust store!), while `DPM.generateKeyPair()` with `StrongBox` (`setIsStrongBoxBacked(true)`) and `ID_TYPE_BASE_INFO | ID_TYPE_SERIAL | ID_TYPE_IMEI | ID_TYPE_INDIVIDUAL_ATTESTATION` mints non-exportable `802.1X EAP-TLS` and Conditional Access client keys inside `Titan M2` / `KeyMint` [467, 471]. Whereas ordinary apps requesting `Tag::ATTESTATION_ID_IMEI` (`714`) receive an immediate `SecurityException` to prevent ad-tech hardware tracking, `DevicePolicyManagerService` authorizes hardware-fused `IMEI` and `Serial` binding inside X.509 `OID .17` exclusively for verified organization-owned `Device Owner` and `COPE` provisioning flows, and then silently pre-grants key aliases to work VPN or browser UIDs via `grantKeyPairToApp()` without interactive `choosePrivateKeyAlias()` dialogs [467, 468, 471].

### `Zero-Touch Enrollment`, `FRP`, and Setup-Wizard Lockdown

How does an enterprise guarantee that a shrink-wrapped `Fully Managed` or `COPE` handset shipped directly to a remote employee (or wiped via `fastboot`/`Recovery` by a thief) cannot bypass enrollment into an unmanaged `User 0` launcher [470, 471]?

Through `Zero-Touch Enrollment` paired with Enterprise `Factory Reset Protection` (`FRP`) and bootloader unlock blocks [122, 470, 471]. When an organization purchases fleet hardware from an authorized B2B carrier or reseller, the reseller uploads each handset's hardware identifiers (`IMEI`, `MEID`, or `(manufacturer, model, serial_number)`) into Google's cloud `Zero-Touch Portal` (`developers.google.com/zero-touch`) bound to the enterprise's `DPC` package URL, SHA-256 APK signature checksum, and provisioning extras JSON [470]. During initial out-of-box boot (and after *every* subsequent `Recovery` factory reset), `SetupWizard` runs while `Settings.Global.DEVICE_PROVISIONED == 0` and `Settings.Secure.USER_SETUP_COMPLETE == 0` suppress the home launcher, notification shade, quick settings tiles, `adb`, and developer options. As soon as Wi-Fi or cellular connectivity comes online, `SetupWizard` queries the Zero-Touch backend over pinned TLS using hardware-attested device identity, downloads and verifies the enterprise `DPC`, enforces `ACTION_ADMIN_POLICY_COMPLIANCE` *before* setting `DEVICE_PROVISIONED = 1` ([467]), and applies `UserManager.DISALLOW_OEM_UNLOCK` so `fastboot flashing unlock` remains permanently disabled in bootloader NVRAM [122, 470]. Simultaneously, `setFactoryResetProtectionPolicy()` writes an explicit allowlist of enterprise IT administrator Google Account numeric `people/me` IDs (broadcasting `com.google.android.gms.auth.FRP_CONFIG_CHANGED` to `com.google.android.gms`) directly into the dedicated raw `persistent_data_block` flash partition outside `/data`: even if an attacker forces an offline wipe while network access to Zero-Touch is blocked, the bootloader and `SetupWizard` refuse setup until an allowlisted IT admin authenticates, while preventing departing employees' personal Google accounts from locking corporate hardware on offboarding [122, 467, 471].

> **Key idea.**
> `Android Enterprise` resolves the classic BYOD privacy-versus-control paradox by pushing isolation down into the OS reference monitor rather than trusting policy promises. Because `User 10` occupies a disjoint `UID` range (`1000000+`), disjoint SELinux `MLS` category (`c522`), disjoint `netd` routing table, disjoint CA store, and disjoint `KeyMint`-backed `fscrypt` key ($K_{\text{CE}, 10}$), an IT administrator holds deterministic cryptographic wipe and DLP control over corporate assets while remaining *mathematically incapable* of decrypting or listing `User 0` personal files, even on company-purchased `COPE` hardware.

## Vendor, OEM, & Custom-OS Boundaries

Google's *Android Enterprise Recommended (`AER`)* validation and NIAP Common Criteria `MDFPP` certifications [402, 467] standardize `Zero-Touch`, `StrongBox` hardware key support, and timely security-patch delivery across enterprise OEMs. Samsung layers `Samsung Knox Platform for Enterprise` (`KPE`) and `Knox Mobile Enrollment` (`KME`) atop AOSP `DevicePolicyManager` [27, 488]: hardware-fused e-fuses (`Knox Warranty Bit`, irreversibly blown from `0x0` to `0x1` if an `ORANGE` or unsigned boot image ever executes) gate `Knox Vault` keys, while `DualDAR` wraps `User 10` files in *two independent cryptographic layers* (an outer hardware key and an inner enterprise-controlled cipher active even in `BFU` before any passcode is entered, meeting classified government data-at-rest mandates without losing `AFU` push notifications) [27, 488].

On `GrapheneOS` (`YELLOW`), although cloud `Zero-Touch` and GMS `AMAPI` are intentionally absent by default, AOSP's underlying multi-user (`up to 32 full users`), `Private Space`, and `Work Profile` kernel engines operate at full strength [20, 474, 479]. Security-conscious `GrapheneOS` practitioners routinely pair secondary user profiles (each with an independent `Weaver`-backed passcode and instant "End Session" `FS_IOC_REMOVE_ENCRYPTION_KEY` eviction!) or open-source local `Profile Owner` controllers (`Shelter` / `Insular`) to cage untrusted regional apps or Sandboxed Google Play inside an isolated `UID` and `MLS` compartment without any cloud EMM server [20, 476].

### Structural Boundary and Handoff to Cross-App Agentic AI

The deepest structural boundary of a `Work Profile` is that `User 0` and `User 10` share one `EL1` host Linux kernel (`containers`, not separate `EL2` `pKVM` virtual machines): whenever `User 10` is actively unlocked in `AFU`, a full `EL1` kernel exploit triggered inside `User 0` can read `CE_10` in memory, whereas pausing the Work Profile (*Quiet Mode*) restores hardware-backed `BFU` confidentiality via `FS_IOC_REMOVE_ENCRYPTION_KEY` [476, 478].

Notice the architectural principle that made safe cross-profile collaboration (`Connected Apps`) possible in `Android Enterprise`: *explicit, consent-gated, typed capability contracts rather than ambient cross-sandbox scraping* [481]. Between 2024 and 2026, Android confronted an even more sweeping cross-application challenge: *on-device and cross-app Agentic AI* asked by users to summarize private notifications across messaging apps and execute multi-step workflows across third-party apps. [Chapter 18](18-on-device-agentic-ai.md) dissects how `AICore`, `Private Compute Core`, `AppFunctions`, and `Secure Virtual Window` tame agentic execution without turning the assistant into an omniscient confused deputy.

## Inspecting via ADB, Fastboot, & Attestation

Enumerate all provisioned users and managed profiles (`UserInfo{0:Owner:...}` vs. `UserInfo{10:Work profile:... MANAGED}`) alongside active `Device Owner` or `Profile Owner` `DPC` bindings and `COPE` organization-ownership flags:

```bash
adb shell pm list users
```
