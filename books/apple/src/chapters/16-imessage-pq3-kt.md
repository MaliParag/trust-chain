# iMessage PQ3 Post-Quantum Ratcheting and Contact Key Verification

## Overview

* **Link in the Chain:** End-to-end encrypted real-time messaging and verifiable key-directory layer in Part IV; protects interpersonal communication across Apple's untrusted `APNs` and `IDS` cloud transport against retrospective chosen-ciphertext attacks, harvest-now/decrypt-later quantum cryptanalysis, and compelled or breached key-server equivocation.
* **Core Security Invariant:** Under `PQ3` (`Level 3` messaging security), every pairwise device session combines `NIST P-256` elliptic-curve Diffie-Hellman keys with initial `ML-KEM-1024` (`Kyber-1024`) encapsulation and periodic `ML-KEM-768` (`Kyber-768`) post-quantum ratchets mixed through dual `HKDF-SHA384` extractions, while `Contact Key Verification` (`KT`) commits `IDS` identity keys into an `ECVRF`-indexed sparse Merkle prefix map audited continuously by user devices.
* **Platform Matrix:** `PQ3` executes automatically by default across `iMessage` conversations where participants run `iOS 17.4+`, `iPadOS 17.4+`, `macOS 14.4+`, `watchOS 10.4+`, and `visionOS 1.1+` (negotiating the highest mutually supported protocol version per pairwise session), while `Contact Key Verification` spans `iOS 17.2+`, `iPadOS 17.2+`, `macOS 14.2+`, `watchOS 10.2+`, and `visionOS` (`PQ3` and `KT` explicitly exclude carrier `SMS` and `RCS` fallbacks).
* **Trusted Computing Base (TCB):** Per-device `SEP`-resident `ECDSA P-256` authentication keys, `identityservicesd` `PQ3` triple-ratchet state machines (`HKDF-SHA384`, `P-256`, `ML-KEM-1024`/`768`, `Padme`, and `AES-256-CTR`), `iCloud Keychain` `Octagon` Account Keys, the `Apple Transparency Service` `VRF` Merkle log and `Signed Mutation Timestamps` (`SMT`s), and client `transparencyd` self-audit daemons.
* **Failure Horizon & Handoff:** Endpoint zero-click memory compromises (*FORCEDENTRY*, *Operation Triangulation*, *DarkSword*) read plaintext `sms.db`/`chat.db` rows after `PQ3` decrypts them in `AFU` RAM, legacy secondary devices signed into an `Apple Account` force `Level 1` copy fan-out, and Standard `iCloud Backup` (unless `ADP` from [Chapter 15](15-icloud-hsm-adp.md) is active) escrows `Messages in iCloud` keys on Apple servers; hands off zero-knowledge offline RF beacon cryptography (`Find My`) to [Chapter 17](17-find-my-anti-tracking.md).

## Threat Model & Design Tension

When Apple launched `iMessage` alongside `iOS 5` in October 2011, it deployed automatic multi-device end-to-end encryption (`E2EE`) across hundreds of millions of endpoints without requiring users to manage PGP keyrings [3, 440]. Under that original 2011 architecture, first dissected by Cyril Cattiaux at Quarkslab in 2013 [441], each enrolled `iPhone`, `iPad`, or `Mac` generated static asymmetric keys, uploaded public keys to the *Apple Identity Directory Service (`IDS`)*, and encrypted outgoing messages pairwise per recipient device over *Apple Push Notification service (`APNs`)* [440, 441]. Against Tier 3 mercenary surveillance and Tier 4 nation-state cryptanalysis, however, three structural flaws gradually rendered first- and second-generation `iMessage` cryptography untenable [3, 15, 442].

First, early `iMessage` (`iOS 5` through `iOS 9.2`) suffered from *static key reuse* and *malleable ciphertext composition* [441, 442]. The v1 client compressed its message plist with `gzip` (`DEFLATE` LZ77 plus Huffman coding), generated a 128-bit `AES` session key, placed 101 bits of that key alongside 59 overflow bytes inside a `1280`-bit `RSA-OAEP` (`RSA-1280`) envelope, encrypted remaining bytes under unauthenticated `AES-128-CTR` (`IV = 1`), and appended an `ECDSA P-256` signature [441, 442]. In 2016, Christina Garman, Matthew Green, Gabriel Kaptchuk, Ian Miers, and Michael Rushanan at Johns Hopkins University (*Dancing on the Lip of the Volcano*, `CVE-2016-1788`) demonstrated a retrospective *chosen-ciphertext attack* on that construction [442, 443]. Because pre-`iOS 9.3` clients verified the `ECDSA` signature against whatever sender URI appeared in the outer `APNs` envelope without binding the original sender inside the encrypted payload, an adversary intercepting a ciphertext could strip the original signature, flip candidate bits in the `AES-128-CTR` stream, and re-sign the mutated packet under a disposable Apple ID [442]. When a message carried an `iCloud` attachment descriptor (containing a download URL and 64-hex-character 256-bit `AES` attachment key compressed via `gzip`), `CRC32` and Huffman decompression checks plus automatic HTTPS fetches acted as a live format oracle, recovering the 256-bit attachment key in fewer than $2^{18}$ (`~262,144`) silent queries [442, 443].

Second, although Apple patched `CVE-2016-1788` in `iOS 9.3` / `OS X 10.11.4` [443] and upgraded `iMessage` in `iOS 13` (`2019`) to `SEP`-protected `NIST P-256` `ECIES` with periodic classical rekeying [3, 440], classical elliptic curves remain vulnerable to *Harvest-Now, Decrypt-Later (`HNDL`)* quantum collection [3, 444]. Peter Shor's polynomial-time quantum algorithm solves both `RSA` factorization and the Elliptic Curve Discrete Logarithm Problem (`ECDLP`) on a cryptanalytically relevant quantum computer (`CRQC`) [444], while Lov Grover's search halves symmetric key strength from $n$ to $n/2$ bits, motivating `AES-256` and `SHA-384` [445]. Adversaries recording `APNs` trunks today need only archive ciphertexts until `CRQC` hardware arrives [3]. Even `Level 2` post-quantum handshakes such as Signal's September 2023 `PQXDH` [446, 447] encapsulate a `Kyber` secret *only once* at session setup while running subsequent `Double Ratchet` turns purely on classical `Curve25519` [3, 448]: if any intermediate session state ever leaks from RAM, a `HNDL` attacker with a future quantum computer breaks every subsequent classical Diffie-Hellman step permanently [3, 449, 450].

Third, transport encryption collapses if senders encrypt to attacker-controlled keys [15, 451]. When Alice messages `bob@icloud.com`, `identityservicesd` queries Apple's `IDS` directory for Bob's registered device keys [440]. Without verifiable transparency, a breached or legally compelled `IDS` server could execute a silent *split-view (equivocation) attack*, returning `[Bob_iPhone, Bob_Mac]` to Bob while returning `[Bob_iPhone, Bob_Mac, Ghost_Agency_Device]` to Alice so Alice's phone establishes a pairwise `PQ3` session directly with the agency's ghost key [15, 451, 452]. Solving both problems at billion-device scale required pairing `PQ3` continuous post-quantum ratcheting with `Contact Key Verification` (`KT`).

## Silicon & System Mechanics

### From RSA-1280 and ECIES to Apple's Four-Level Messaging Taxonomy

Understanding why `PQ3` is structured as a hybrid triple-ratchet protocol starts with tracing the exact cryptographic evolution across `iMessage` protocol generations from 2011 to the present [3, 441, 442, 449]:

| Protocol Generation | Deployment Era | Confidentiality & Key Agreement | Symmetric Cipher & Padding | Sender Authentication & Key Custody | Apple SEAR Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `iMessage` Legacy (`RSA`) | `iOS 5` - `iOS 12.4` (`2011`-`2019`) | Static per-device `RSA-1280` `OAEP` key transport [441] | `AES-128-CTR` over `gzip` (`CVE-2016-1788` oracle pre-`9.3`) [442, 443] | Software `ECDSA P-256` keypair; blind `IDS` `TOFU` directory | `Level 1` |
| `iMessage` `ECIES` + Rekey | `iOS 13` - `iOS 17.3` (`2019`-`2024`) | `SEP`-backed `NIST P-256` `ECIES` + periodic classical rekeying [3, 440] | `AES-128-CTR` + `HMAC-SHA256` | `SEP` hardware `ECDSA P-256` key; `KT` added in `17.2+` [15] | `Level 1` |
| Signal `PQXDH` Reference | External (`Sep 2023+`) | Hybrid `X25519` + `ML-KEM-1024` *initial handshake only*; classical `X25519` Double Ratchet thereafter [446, 447, 448] | `AES-256-CBC` + `HMAC-SHA256` | Software `XEdDSA` identity key (shared or transferred) | `Level 2` |
| `iMessage` `PQ3` | `iOS 17.4+` / `macOS 14.4+` (`Feb 2024+`) | Hybrid `P-256` + `ML-KEM-1024` init *plus* continuous `P-256` + periodic `ML-KEM-768` KEM ratcheting [3, 14] | `AES-256-CTR` + `HKDF-SHA384` + `Padme` $O(\log\log M)$ padding [3, 453] | Hardware `SEP` `ECDSA P-256` per-message sig + `KT` `Account Key` [3, 15] | `Level 3` |

In February 2024, Apple SEAR formalized this four-tier hierarchy to clarify what "post-quantum messaging" actually protects against [3]. `Level 0` systems omit default end-to-end encryption altogether, leaving cloud providers in custody of plaintext transcripts. `Level 1` systems deploy classical E2EE (`RSA` or elliptic-curve `X3DH` and `Double Ratchet` [448]), resisting classical wiretaps while exposing all archived traffic to future `CRQC` decryption under `HNDL` [444]. `Level 2` systems (`PQXDH` [446], formally verified by Karthikeyan Bhargavan, Charlie Jacomme, Franziskus Kiefer, and Rolfe Schmidt at Cryspen and Signal [447]) mix a `NIST FIPS 203` module-lattice KEM [14] into the initial session root key $RK_0$, defeating passive `HNDL` *provided no intermediate ratchet state is ever leaked*. `Level 3`, achieved at global consumer scale first by `PQ3` [3], requires post-quantum cryptography in *both* asynchronous 0-RTT session initialization and ongoing in-band ratcheting so that a conversation automatically self-heals (*post-compromise security*, or `PCS`) even against an adversary possessing both a point-in-time RAM dump and a future quantum computer [3, 449, 450].

### PQ3 Prekey Registration, Dual-Extract Hybrid Init, and the Triple Ratchet

Because `iMessage` delivers messages across multi-device accounts (`Alice` owns $m$ devices; `Bob` owns $n$ devices), `identityservicesd` establishes $m + n - 1$ *independent pairwise `PQ3` sessions* linking Alice's sending device to each of Bob's $n$ devices and each of Alice's other $m-1$ synced devices [3, 440]. Compromising the session state on an old laptop therefore reveals nothing about pairwise ratchet states negotiated exclusively between two phones.

![Figure: iMessage PQ3 Level-3 protocol architecture combining P-256 + ML-KEM-768 initial hybrid key agreement with per-message symmetric chains and periodic ML-KEM-1024 post-quantum healing ratchets.](diagrams/16-pq3-hybrid-ratchet.svg)

To support asynchronous 0-RTT messaging when a recipient device is offline, every `PQ3`-capable device periodically uploads an `IDS` prekey bundle containing two public encryption keys: a classical `NIST P-256` ECDH prekey $pk_{\text{EC}}$ (`33` compressed bytes) and a post-quantum `Kyber-1024` (`ML-KEM-1024`, Category 5, `1,568`-byte public key [3, 14]) encapsulation prekey $pk_{\text{PQ}}$ [3, 449]. Those prekeys carry an expiration timestamp and `PQ3` protocol version attribute signed via `ECDSA P-256` by the device's non-exportable `SEP` authentication key $IK_{\text{SEP}}$, which in turn carries a signature from the user's `KT` Account Key $AK_{\text{KT}}$, blocking `IDS` downgrade attacks back to `Level 1` [3, 15, 450].

When Alice's device initiates a `PQ3` session to Bob's device, `identityservicesd` validates Bob's `KT` and `SEP` signatures, generates an ephemeral `P-256` keypair $(ek_A, EPK_A = ek_A G)$, computes $ss_{\text{EC}} = \text{ECDH}(ek_A, pk_{\text{EC}})$, and invokes `ML-KEM-1024.Encaps`$(pk_{\text{PQ}}) \to (ct_0, ss_{\text{PQ}})$, producing a `1,568`-byte ciphertext $ct_0$ and 32-byte shared secret $ss_{\text{PQ}}$ [3, 14, 449]. Rather than concatenating raw shared secrets into one hash call, `PQ3` applies a *chained `HKDF-SHA384-Extract` hybrid combiner* [3, 449]:

$$K_1 = \text{HKDF-SHA384-Extract}\bigl(\text{salt}_0,\; ss_{\text{EC}}\bigr), \qquad K_2 = \text{HKDF-SHA384-Extract}\bigl(K_1,\; ss_{\text{PQ}}\bigr)$$

$$SK_0 = \text{HKDF-SHA384-Extract}\bigl(K_2,\; \text{"PQ3-Init"} \;\|\; \text{Transcript}\bigl(\text{ID}_A, \text{ID}_B, EPK_A, pk_{\text{EC}}, pk_{\text{PQ}}, ct_0\bigr)\bigr)$$

Because `HKDF-SHA384-Extract` (`48`-byte output resilient to Grover's bound [445]) acts as a dual pseudorandom function (`dual-PRF`), $SK_0$ remains indistinguishable from uniform 384-bit noise unless an attacker *simultaneously* solves `Gap-ECDH` on `P-256` *and* breaks `IND-CCA2` security of `ML-KEM-1024`, while transcript binding prevents unknown-key-share substitution [3, 449, 450].

Once $SK_0$ is established, ongoing `PQ3` traffic advances three nested ratchets in tandem [3]:

First, the *Symmetric Ratchet* runs on *every message* $(j \to j+1)$, deriving a 256-bit message key $MK_j$ and advancing the chain key via `HKDF-SHA384` before zeroizing $MK_j$ immediately upon decryption for instant forward secrecy [3, 449]. Second, the *Classical `NIST P-256` ECDH Ratchet* attaches a 32-byte compressed ephemeral point to every direction flip, mixing fresh classical DH entropy into session state at minimal wire cost [3, 448].

Third, the *Periodic `ML-KEM-768` Post-Quantum KEM Ratchet* delivers `Level 3` quantum post-compromise healing [3, 449, 450]. Whereas static `IDS` prekeys use `ML-KEM-1024`, in-session ratchet keys are single-use ephemerals instantiated with `ML-KEM-768` (`Kyber-768`, Category 3, `1,184`-byte public key plus `1,088`-byte ciphertext = `2,272` bytes) [3, 14]. To amortize that `> 2 KB` header over cellular links, `PQ3` triggers `ML-KEM-768` re-encapsulation adaptively approximately every *50 outgoing messages* or at least once every *7 days* [3]. Even when a recipient reads a notification without replying, `iMessage`'s *encrypted delivery receipts* automatically return the peer's ratchet response whenever the device is online, completing both `P-256` and `ML-KEM-768` healing round-trips without waiting for human input [3].

Before payload encryption, `PQ3` obscures plaintext length side channels using the *Padme* padding algorithm introduced by Nikitin, Barman, Lueks, Underwood, Hubaux, and Ford (`PETS 2019`) [3, 453]. Naive block padding leaks exact sentence lengths, whereas padding to the next power of two wastes up to 100% bandwidth; `Padme` masks the low mantissa bits of message length $L$ so padded lengths leak at most $O(\log \log M)$ bits of size information across messages up to maximum length $M$ while capping byte overhead at $\le 12\%$ (and far less on media chunks) [453]. The `Padme`-padded cleartext is then encrypted under `AES-256-CTR` using key and `IV` material expanded via `HKDF-SHA384` from $MK_j$, and the entire wire packet (covering sender and recipient URIs, `APNs` push tokens, `AES-256-CTR` ciphertext, authenticated header data, ratchet public keys, and a *ratchet-derived message key indicator* pinning the exact ratchet coordinate) is signed with `ECDSA P-256` inside the sender's `SEP` [3, 449].

### Dual Machine-Checked Proofs: Symbolic Tamarin and Computational Reductions

Designing custom hybrid triple-ratchet protocols by hand is notoriously error-prone: subtle interactions between asynchronous 0-RTT prekeys, out-of-order message delivery, multi-device fan-out, and partial state exposure routinely harbor key-compromise impersonation (`KCI`) or cross-session replay bugs. Apple SEAR therefore made formal verification a release gate for `PQ3`, commissioning two orthogonal mathematical proofs published alongside `iOS 17.4` [3, 449, 450].

In the *symbolic model*, Felix Linker, Ralf Sasse, and David Basin at ETH Zurich modeled `PQ3` inside the `TAMARIN` multiset-rewriting theorem prover [450, 454]. Symbolic verification treats cryptographic primitives as idealized algebraic functions while granting an active network attacker unbounded concurrency, arbitrary packet dropping/reordering, and fine-grained *corruption rules* capable of selectively leaking long-term `SEP` authentication keys, `IDS` prekeys, or transient ratchet states alongside a quantum adversary rule that inverts any classical `ECDH` exchange instantaneously [450]. `TAMARIN` produced machine-checked proofs of both *forward secrecy* and *post-quantum post-compromise security*: if Alice or Bob suffers a transient session-state leak at epoch $k$, secrecy of all messages is deterministically restored as soon as one uncompromised `ML-KEM-768` healing step completes, and even if the Application Processor (`AP`) is temporarily compromised (allowing the attacker to ask `SEP` to sign packets while resident), rebooting or updating the OS evicts the attacker from `AP` RAM and immediately terminates impersonation because the `ECDSA P-256` private scalar never leaves `SEP` silicon [3, 450].

In the *computational model*, Douglas Stebila at the University of Waterloo constructed reductionist game-hopping security proofs [449] (complementing the mechanized `CryptoVerif` methodology pioneered by Bruno Blanchet and applied by Cryspen to `PQXDH` [447, 455]). Stebila decomposed `PQ3`'s `HKDF-SHA384` key schedule across multi-stage hybrid games, proving that derived `AES-256-CTR` message keys remain computationally indistinguishable from random bitstrings against both classical and quantum polynomial-time adversaries so long as `HMAC-SHA384` satisfies `dual-PRF` security and *at least one* of `NIST P-256` (against classical attackers) or `ML-KEM-1024`/`ML-KEM-768` (against quantum attackers) remains `IND-CCA` secure [449]. Notice also why `PQ3` intentionally retains classical `ECDSA P-256` for message and `KT` signatures today rather than inflating every single text message with multi-kilobyte `ML-DSA` (`Dilithium`) signatures [3]: unlike confidentiality (which is broken *retroactively* by `HNDL` against archived ciphertexts), breaking authentication requires forging a signature *contemporaneously during live session negotiation*, long before `CRQC` hardware exists [3, 449].

### Contact Key Verification (KT): VRF Prefix Merkle Trees, SMTs, and Octagon

`PQ3` secures the pairwise cryptographic channel, while *iMessage Contact Key Verification (`KT`)* secures key discovery against `IDS` directory compromise [15, 456].

![Figure: Contact Key Verification (KT) sequence anchoring IDS public-key lookups in an append-only VRF-indexed Merkle map continuously self-audited by recipient devices.](diagrams/16-contact-key-verification-kt.svg)

Why couldn't Apple simply copy Web PKI *Certificate Transparency (`CT`, `RFC 9162`)* [457] directly onto `IDS`? First, `CT` logs append X.509 certificates into a flat chronological Merkle list where verifying *non-existence* or auditing *all* current keys for `alice@icloud.com` requires scanning the entire multi-billion-entry log! Second, publishing plaintext phone numbers and email addresses in a public Merkle tree would create a global directory-harvesting goldmine for spammers and authoritarian regimes; third, whereas Google's largest `CT` log (`Xenon 2023`) accumulated roughly 2 billion entries over *four years*, Apple's `IDS` key directory ingests *more than 2 billion device-key updates every single week* [15, 457]!

To solve privacy, lookup efficiency, and billion-user churn simultaneously, `KT` synthesizes academic *Key Transparency* breakthroughs from `CONIKS` (`Melara et al., 2015` [451]), `SEEMless` (`Chase et al., 2019` [452]), and `Parakeet` / WhatsApp's `Auditable Key Directory` (`Malvai et al., 2023` [458]) into a *Verifiable Log-Backed Map* managed by the `Apple Transparency Service` and verified on-device by `transparencyd` [15]:

![Figure: Contact Key Verification (KT) VRF-indexed sparse prefix Merkle map combining RFC 9381 ECVRF index blinding, 48-hour Signed Mutation Timestamps, piggybacked STH gossip, and Octagon self-auditing.](diagrams/16-extra-kt-vrf-merkle-tree.svg)

Examine the four cryptographic mechanisms interlocking inside Figure 3 [15, 451, 459, 460]:

First, *`ECVRF` Privacy-Preserving Indexing (`RFC 9381`)* [459]: to map an `iMessage` URI $u$ (such as `tel:+14155550199` or `mailto:bob@icloud.com`) into a 256-bit binary prefix-tree coordinate without exposing $u$ to external log auditors, the `KT` server evaluates an Elliptic Curve Verifiable Random Function under its VRF private key $sk_{\text{VRF}}$, computing pseudorandom output $\beta = \text{ECVRF}_{sk_{\text{VRF}}}(u)$, deterministic proof $\pi_{\text{VRF}}$, and leaf index $idx = \text{SHA-256}(\beta)$ [15, 459]. Anyone holding `KT`'s public key $pk_{\text{VRF}}$ and knowing $u$ verifies $\text{ECVRF\_verify}(pk_{\text{VRF}}, u, \beta, \pi_{\text{VRF}}) = \text{VALID}$, guaranteeing that each URI maps to *exactly one unique, unforgeable leaf slot* in the sparse prefix tree, while observers lacking $u$ see only random 256-bit hashes and salted commitments [451, 459].

Second, *Signed Mutation Timestamps (`SMT`s) and 48-Hour `MMD` Auditing* [15]: batching $>2\times 10^9$ weekly mutations into global sparse Merkle map revisions takes minutes, yet a user unboxing a brand-new `iPhone` expects `iMessage` to work within milliseconds. Whenever `IDS` registers a new device key signed by the user's `Account Key`, the `Apple Transparency Service` immediately issues a cryptographically signed promise called an `SMT` [15]. Peer devices accept a fresh key backed by a valid `SMT` immediately *and* cache that `SMT` locally so `transparencyd` verifies on-device that the promised leaf actually merges into the global Merkle map within a hard *48-hour Maximum Merge Delay (`MMD`)* [15, 457].

Third, *On-Device Consistency Proofs, Encrypted In-Band `STH` Gossip, and `CloudKit`/`Octagon` Self-Auditing* [11, 15, 421]: unlike WhatsApp's `AKD` (which relies primarily on external auditors to check epoch-to-epoch log consistency [15, 458]), Apple's `Messages` and `transparencyd` daemons verify append-only Merkle consistency proofs directly on user devices *and* gossip observed Signed Tree Head (`STH`) hashes peer-to-peer by embedding `STH` digests inside the `PQ3`-encrypted body of a small random fraction of ordinary `iMessage` texts [15]! If a compromised `KT` server ever attempted a split-view fork (serving Tree $T_A$ to Alice and Tree $T_B$ to Bob), encrypted peer-to-peer gossip exposes the conflicting `STH` signatures immediately. Simultaneously, every device belonging to Alice periodically queries `IDS` and `KT` for Alice's own URIs and cross-checks the logged device keys and `KT` opt-in state against both her `SEP`-anchored `Octagon` account circle ([Chapter 15](15-icloud-hsm-adp.md)) and a dedicated *end-to-end encrypted `CloudKit` container* unreadable and unmodifiable by Apple [11, 15, 421]. Because legitimate new Macs or iPads sign their `IDS` keys with the `Octagon`-synced `ECDSA` `Account Key` stored in `iCloud Keychain`, normal device upgrades validate silently with *zero false-positive UI noise*, whereas any unauthorized ghost key injected by `IDS` triggers a persistent Settings badge and inline conversation transcript warning [15, 456].

Fourth, *Vaudenay `SAS` Codes and Static `APVR` Contact Pinning* [15, 456, 460]: to defeat even a first-contact (`TOFU`) man-in-the-middle attack launched *before* background self-auditing runs, two `KT`-enrolled users can tap *Verify Contact* to execute the *Serge Vaudenay Short Authenticated Strings (`SAS`, `CRYPTO 2005`)* commitment protocol [460], comparing a short 8-digit decimal code live over voice or in person (saving the verified `Account Key` hash into E2EE `CloudKit` tied to the peer's Contact card across all future device replacements!), or publish a static `APVR...` *Public Verification Code* on social profiles and business cards so journalists and officials are pinned from message zero [15, 456].

## Platform Forks Across Apple OSes

Across `iOS`, `iPadOS`, `macOS`, `watchOS`, and `visionOS`, `identityservicesd` and `transparencyd` share one unified C++/Objective-C/Swift cryptographic core, yet multi-device account topology creates three decisive security boundaries in practice [3, 11, 15, 456]. First, because `iMessage` encrypts pairwise per device, the *oldest active device signed into a participant's `Apple Account` sets the confidentiality ceiling for its copy of the transcript*: if Bob converses on an `iPhone 17` running `iOS 26` (`PQ3` `Level 3`) while leaving a 2016 `MacBook` on `macOS Monterey 12` (`Level 1` `ECIES`) signed into `iMessage` in a desk drawer, Alice's phone sends a `PQ3` ciphertext to Bob's `iPhone 17` *and* a classical `Level 1` `ECIES` ciphertext of the exact same message to Bob's `macOS 12` laptop [3]! For that reason, enabling `Contact Key Verification` enforces a strict account-wide floor: `KT` refuses activation (or pauses/warns) until *every* device signed into `iCloud` and `iMessage` runs at least `iOS 17.2`, `iPadOS 17.2`, `macOS 14.2`, `watchOS 10.2`, or `visionOS 1.1` [15, 456].

Second, `Apple Watch` (`watchOS`) participates natively in both `PQ3` and `KT` using its own on-die `SEP` authentication key, whether tethered over Bluetooth to a companion `iPhone` or operating standalone over LTE, whereas green-bubble `SMS`, `MMS`, and carrier `RCS` threads bypass `IDS`, `PQ3`, and `KT` entirely, traversing telecom SS7/IMS relays outside Apple's trust chain [3]. Third, transport security interacts directly with cloud storage custody from [Chapter 15](15-icloud-hsm-adp.md): while `Messages in iCloud` encrypts its `CloudKit` zone end-to-end when `iCloud Backup` is disabled, enabling *Standard* `iCloud Backup` places a copy of the `Messages in iCloud` recovery key inside the server-escrowed backup blob unless the user turns on *Advanced Data Protection (`ADP`)* [11]!
